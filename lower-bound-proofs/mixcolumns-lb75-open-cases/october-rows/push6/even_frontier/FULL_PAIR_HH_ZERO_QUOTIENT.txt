F5. EXACT FINITE STATEMENT

Let H1,H2 be any two distinct full-helper candidate values for the transpose
boundary B, and let T1,T2 be their chosen boundary-triple forms.  Put
d=H1+H2 and Fd=T1+T2.  Let a,b be actual even values, each represented by
either a boundary pair or a repeated-helper triple plus one boundary
coordinate.  Thus a,b have forms Fa,Fb of support at most four.

Whenever d+a+b=0, the boundary relation

                              Fd+Fa+Fb

lies in R10.  This includes arbitrary cancellations of boundary coordinates.

The statement uses complete candidate sets forced by the boundary, not a
restricted library of permitted circuit masks.  An actual r=3 helper is
necessarily one of the 112 full candidates.  An actual repeated-bright
label is necessarily one of the complete repeated-helper-plus-boundary
values, though a candidate value need not have that incidence in a circuit.

FINITE PROOF AND COVERAGE

Let R be all 2,016 conceivable boundary-pair values.  Let P be all values
H+B for candidates H with at least two boundary-triple forms, excluding R;
|P|=30,340.  Every value of P has a chosen boundary four-form.

Changing the triple form of a helper changes Fd by a relation of support
at most six, in R10.  Changing a chosen four-form of an actual color
changes Fa or Fb by a relation of support at most eight, also in R10.
Therefore one chosen form per value suffices for the quotient calculation.

First cover the cases that do NOT need a finite EE search.  Even if d is
DARK in the actual circuit, its mask may belong to R or P; actual incidence
and conceivable representations must not be confused.

  * If d belongs to R, its actual HH six-form differs from the conceivable
    pair by a relation of support <=8.  Replace it modulo R10 by that
    pair; the remaining EE relation has support <=2+4+4=10.

  * If d belongs to P, its actual HH six-form differs from the conceivable
    four-form by a relation of support <=10.  Replace it modulo R10 by
    that four-form.  If a or b has a pair-form, the remaining row has
    support <=10; otherwise all three colors belong to P and F1 applies.

No actual BB reference or actual repeated HB contact for d is asserted
or required in these two substitutions.

There remain exactly 4,608 possible d values outside P union R, arising
from 4,800 unordered pairs of full-helper candidates.  Of these d values,
4,416 have one candidate full pair and 192 have two; ALL 4,800 pair pivots
are checked separately.  The distinct d count alone must not be mistaken
for uniqueness of its helper pair.

For each pivot Fd, enumerate every unordered a,b in P with a+b=d, and
every a in R,b in P with a+b=d.  The checker tests respectively

                917,376 P/P alternatives,

EVERY corresponding full 64-coordinate row reduces to ZERO against the

CONSEQUENCE FOR ONE EXCEPTIONAL COLOR

Assume every helper is anchored and every HH endpoint is full.  Suppose
there is exactly one exceptional missing label d.

If d is bright, every even label has a four-form, all non-EE equations
have support <=10, and F2 bounds the entire exceptional EE quotient space
by two.  This case is impossible for every q and y.

If d is dark and lies on an HH edge, pivot it as the six-form from that
edge.  The inherited full-helper incompatibility permits at most one
full/full HH edge of a fixed color.  Thus all other HH rows have known
four-form labels and are short.  Every EE row involving d is now in R10
by F5; all other EE rows are good by F1.  The complete boundary rank32
cannot fit in R10 of rank29.  This case is also impossible for every q,y.

Therefore a lone exceptional color in this incidence class must be DARK
and occur ONLY in EE equations.  It needs at least FOUR such equations:
one eliminates the color coordinate, and at least three remaining
independent rows must supply the quotient dimensions.  In particular,

                 one exception ==> y>=4.                 (F5a)

It also needs at least SEVEN missing colors in total.  The fixed-d EE
matching and restricted-six capacity prove this directly.  Write qB=q-1
for the other, repeated-bright labels.  If an RR pivot is available, all
RB alternatives are short and three BB alternatives need six bright
labels.  If an RB pivot is chosen, it consumes one bright label; three
dimensions require at least two BB alternatives plus a nonzero remaining
RB difference.  These use at least 1+4+1=6 distinct bright labels.  If all
pivots are BB, four BB equations are needed, using eight bright labels.
Thus qB>=6, so

                 one exception ==> q>=7.                 (F5b)

The RR/RB/BB matching follows from the actual value equation d=a+b: two
distinct such EE equations cannot share either other label.  F3 limits
ALL RB differences, not just one arbitrarily selected pair, to rank one.

STRONGER SCALAR CUT

The previous theorem with all helpers anchored and all HH endpoints full
therefore needs at least two exceptional labels whenever

                         q<=6 OR y<=3.                   (C)

Let h0 be the number of helpers with r=0 and W=sum_(r=1 or r=2)v.  The
same integer incidence case split used for the q<=5 theorem now gives,
for EVERY transpose circuit satisfying (C),

                     A-2h0-W <= q-2,
                 Z+E-6h0-3W <= 3(q-2).                   (F5c)

When h0=W=0, two exceptions give p<=q-2.  When h0=0,W>=1, the earlier
all-anchored obstruction gives at least one exception, so p<=q-1.
When h0>=1, the generic p<=q suffices.  These imply p<=q-2+2h0+W;
then A<=p and Z+E<=3p give (F5c).  The final rows are linear, even
though their proof starts with integer circuit incidences.

This strengthens the condition under which the previously audited S1/S2
rows apply.  It does not alter the separately proved matching-budget
rows or make the all-bright conditional theorem unconditional.

LIMITS

F5 handles one full/full HH-derived label together with two pair/four-form
labels.  It does not assert that arbitrary EE equations with two or three
dark HH-derived labels have zero quotient.  Nor does it claim that every
remaining aggregate point is realizable or that a complete global lower
bound has already increased.
