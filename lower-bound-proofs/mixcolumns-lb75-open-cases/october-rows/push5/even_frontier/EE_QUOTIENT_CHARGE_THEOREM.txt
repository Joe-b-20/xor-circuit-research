Current consolidated theorem, 22 October 2026

RESULTS AND SCOPE

The following statements apply to arbitrary normalized LIVE two-input XOR
circuits for AES MixColumns TRANSPOSE. Depth, cancellation, masks, fanout,
and output reuse are unrestricted. They are not claims about a fixed
control-circuit parent inventory.

  * Existing boundary-rank cuts strengthen by replacing y, all EE gates,
    with y_bad, only those EE equations touching an exceptional color.

  * With no helper--helper edges, zero exceptional colors are impossible.
    Exactly one exceptional BRIGHT color is impossible for every q.

  * With no helper--helper edges, exactly one exceptional DARK color
    requires at least seven missing colors in total: q>=7.

  * Let A=sum_(r=3)u be full-helper missing-color incidences,
    Z=sum(r-1)_+u the repeated-helper excess, and
    E=sum_(r=3)max(u-2,0) the full-helper overflow. Let h0 count
    helpers with r=0. For ARBITRARY z,

      A-h0<=q-1 and Z+E-3h0<=3(q-1).

    In addition,

      z=0                 ==> A<=q-1 and Z+E<=3(q-1);
      z=0 and q<=6        ==> A<=q-2 and Z+E<=3(q-2);
      z=0 and all missing colors bright ==> A<=q-3.

  * The saved SURVIVING_MODEL_POINT_75.json transpose configuration is
    excluded as an actual circuit, including its entire incidence class.

These are necessary structural constraints. They do not exclude all
75-gate profiles and do not raise the unconditional numerical lower bound

1. DEFINITIONS AND ELIMINATION

The 64 boundary signals B are the 32 inputs and 32 outputs of M^T. Their
formal relation space has dimension 32. R10 denotes the span of its
dependencies of weight at most ten; dim R10=29.

Normalize the circuit to distinct nonzero signal values, all live. Its
G defining equations are independent in formal signal coordinates: the
latest gate variable in any nonempty sum cannot cancel. They span the
whole evaluation kernel, since the inputs give evaluation rank 32.
Eliminating every nonboundary variable therefore leaves exactly 32
independent boundary relations.

The odd-signal graph has B and all nonoutput odd helpers as vertices.
Every OO or OE defining equation is an edge between its two odd signals,
labeled by its even signal. Let x,y count OO and EE gates, h odd helpers,
and z helper--helper edges. An even label is represented when its ACTUAL
graph contains a B--B edge; q counts the other, missing labels.

At helper i, r_i counts represented-color H--B edges, u_i missing-color
H--B edges, and v_i H--H edges. It is anchored when r_i>0. A missing label
is bright if it has an H--B edge at an anchored helper; otherwise dark.
It is repeated-bright if one such edge has r_i>=2, and full-bright if one
has r_i=3. The exceptional set Q_* contains the missing labels that are
NOT repeated-bright. Thus it contains dark labels and any bright label
whose anchored contacts all have r=1.

An EE equation uses three distinct even labels: its result and parents.
It is good if all three are represented or repeated-bright; otherwise
bad. Let y_bad be the number of bad EE equations.

The standard pivots give boundary forms:

  represented label: 2, using an actual B--B reference;
  anchored helper: 3, using a represented H--B edge;
  bright label: at most 4, using an anchored H--B edge;
  unanchored helper with a bright contact: at most 5.

These are support upper bounds; repeated boundary coordinates cancel.
After the first three pivot types, all remaining anchored H--B rows have
weight at most eight. After the last type, the unanchored helper's other
bright rows have weight at most ten.

Distinguish ACTUAL represented colors from CONCEIVABLE boundary-pair
values. A missing label can equal a boundary-pair value without having an
actual B--B reference. If it has a chosen four-form, that form differs from
the pair by a dependency of weight at most six. Hence it can be replaced
by the pair modulo R10 when bounding quotient rank.

2. COMPLETE FIXED-BOUNDARY CAPACITIES

Let P be the set of H+B values where H has at least two boundary-triple
representations, with all conceivable boundary-pair values removed.
Let P3 use helpers with three boundary-triple representations instead.
For the transpose boundary:

  repeated helpers: 616; their triple forms: 1,344; |P|=30,340;
  strong helpers:   112; their triple forms:   336; |P3|=6,160.

A helper with actual r>=2 belongs to the complete repeated-helper set;
one with actual r=3 belongs to the strong set. These are complete
consequences of the fixed boundary, not guessed intermediate vocabularies.

The complete boundary code has 736 weight-twelve words nonzero modulo
R10. Its complete weight<=12 counts are 84,276,1300,8112 at weights
6,8,10,12; the corresponding cumulative ranks are 28,29,29,32.

F1. THREE REPEATED COLORS GIVE ZERO QUOTIENT RANK.

Every EE relation whose three labels are represented or repeated-bright
lies in R10 after the pivots. A represented or conceivable-pair label
gives support at most 2+4+4=10. Otherwise a nonzero quotient relation
would be a disjoint 4+4+4 split with all three actual colors in P.

Exhaust all 4,250,400 unordered splits of the 736 words. The counts of
splits with respectively 0,1,2,3 colors in P are

  3,361,744; 763,992; 124,664; ZERO.

Therefore every good EE equation lies in R10.

F2. ONE EXCEPTIONAL FOUR-FORM COLOR HAS CAPACITY TWO.

Fix any actual w having a boundary four-form. Consider all nonzero
quotient relations w+a+b=0 with a,b in P, using their four-forms. Group
the complete score-two 4+4+4 splits by the third actual color w. There
are 5,143 active w: 2,599 have quotient rank one and 2,544 rank two;
none has rank three. All other w have rank zero. Changing a four-form
changes a relation by a dependency of weight at most eight, in R10.

The analogous P3 spaces have 228 active w, ALL of rank one.

F3. RESTRICTED SIX-FORMS HAVE CAPACITY ONE.

A restricted form has size at most four, or has size six and contains
a triple whose actual value has at least two boundary-triple forms.
For every actual even d, the differences among all restricted forms
for d have quotient rank at most ONE modulo R10; call the space K_P(d).

A nonzero difference must be two disjoint six-forms. Exhaust all 340,032
unordered 6+6 splits and require each half to contain a repeated-helper
triple. Exactly 47,184 splits qualify, yielding 8,804 active d, every one
of rank one. No rank-two value exists. The strong-helper variant has
2,880 qualifying splits and 320 active d, again all rank one.

Adding a represented pair or two boundary endpoints to a repeated-bright
four-form gives a repeated-helper triple plus three boundary values. If
its weight is six, the triple is contained in it; if cancellation occurs,
its weight is at most four. Thus all such forms are restricted.

F4. TWO EXCEPTIONS AGAINST FULL-BRIGHT COLORS HAVE CAPACITY AT MOST TWO.

For any two actual four-form colors w1,w2 outside P3, consider the sum
of their two individual F2/P3 spaces and all relations w1+w2+a=0 with
a in P3. Each individual space has rank at most one. The actual pair
w1,w2 forces a uniquely; its quotient relation is independent of the
chosen four-forms.

Group all score-one P3 4+4+4 splits by their two exceptional actual
colors, then adjoin both individual spaces. There are 47,184 active
pairs, and EVERY resulting joint space has rank ONE. Pairs without
an active pair relation have rank at most two from the individual
spaces. Therefore the joint rank is universally at most two. If either
exception happens to belong to P3, the one-exception case suffices.

F1,F2,F4 are reconstructed by bright_ee_triples.py; F3 by
repeated_six_forms.py. The root independent audit rebuilds the entire
short code from both halves and independently checks ALL four finite

  push5/root/audit_bright_ee.py
  push5/root/BRIGHT_EE_INDEPENDENT_AUDIT.json

The final independent report is PASS and agrees with every count above.
The separate logical audit of the pivots, one-dark cases, two-exception

3. GENERAL CONSTRUCTOR RANK CUTS

The inherited DARK_LABEL_RANK elimination strengthens to

  3 <= y_bad + sum_(r=0)(u+v-1) + sum_(r=1)min(v,q-u),        (1)

  9 <= 3y_bad + 2z + sum_(r=0)(3u+v-3).                     (2)

Proof. Let h0 count unanchored helpers, U0 their missing H--B incidences,
z0 the H--H edges touching them, and qA the missing labels retained at
anchored H--B or anchored--anchored H--H rows. Retain all represented
anchored-helper rows, all anchored missing H--B rows, all H--H rows with
two anchored endpoints, and ALL GOOD EE rows. Pivot represented labels,
then one represented triple per anchored helper, then one retained row
per retained missing label.

Every good EE row uses only colors already eliminated by these pivots.
It therefore adds an independent boundary row in R10, consuming no new
missing-label pivot. The number of retained boundary rows is

             32-y_bad+h0-U0-z0+(q-qA).                    (3)

The new rows contribute no rank outside R10. The inherited complete
six-form capacity theorem and repeated-helper pair fact bound that rank
by the number z1 of retained H--H edges touching an r=1 helper. An active
color is missing and cannot use any of that helper's u H--B colors;
colors are matchings. Hence z1<=sum_(r=1)min(v,q-u). Combining (3) with
rank R10=29 and z0<=sum_(r=0)v proves (1).

Alternatively, the inherited capacity-two bound gives at most
min(2,m-1)<=2m/3 dimensions for a label on m retained H--H edges.
Thus quotient rank<=2(z-z0)/3. Substitution in (3), discarding q-qA>=0,
and z0<=sum_(r=0)v prove (2).

For an even label e, let d_e be gate-parent fanout and m_e its odd-graph
degree. Its exact number of EE-equation incidences is

                     t_e=d_e-m_e+1.                      (4)

Its odd-graph degree counts OE uses plus its own defining edge if OO-born;
its EE incidence counts EE uses plus its own equation if EE-born. Thus

  y_bad <= sum_(e in Q_*)(d_e-m_e+1)
        <= sum_(e in Q_*)(4+2f_e-m_e),                    (5)

where f_e is its canonical reverse EE count and d_e<=3+2f_e is the
inherited fanout inequality. Multiple exceptional colors in one equation
only overcount it in (5), which is safe.

4. ZERO EXCEPTIONS AND ONE WEAK BRIGHT EXCEPTION

Assume z=0. If Q_* is empty, all missing colors are repeated-bright.
Every unanchored helper has a bright edge and can be pivoted to a
five-form. All remaining non-EE rows have weight at most ten, and all
EE rows are good by F1. The full boundary rank 32 cannot fit in R10.
Thus Q_* cannot be empty.

If Q_* consists of one WEAK BRIGHT color w, all missing colors are
bright, so the same helper pivots make all non-EE rows short. An EE
relation can leave R10 only if it uses w and two repeated-bright colors:
a represented color would give weight<=10. Their entire quotient space
has rank at most two by F2. This again cannot supply the required three
dimensions. This class is impossible for every q and y.

5. EXACTLY ONE DARK EXCEPTION

Assume z=0 and Q_*={d}, where d is dark. Every other missing color is
repeated-bright; write qB=q-1 for their number. Let k count unanchored
helpers incident to d, and t the number of distinct bright colors
sharing such a helper with d.

A live nonoutput odd helper has graph degree at least two. With z=0,
an unanchored helper has only missing-color H--B edges. Each color is
a matching, so one dark color can supply at most one of them. Hence
every unanchored helper has a bright edge and can be pivoted to a
five-form.

No two helpers can share both d and a bright b. Each would represent
d+b by its two boundary neighbors. Sidonicity forces the same unordered
pair. The same assignment gives equal helper values; the swapped
assignment makes the four H--B gate equations sum to zero. Both are
forbidden. Therefore touched bright sets at different dark-incident
helpers are disjoint, and t is the sum of their bright degrees. In
particular t>=k. For a canonical reverse, the inherited degree-three
lemma gives t>=2k.

CASE k>=1.

Pivot each unanchored helper through a bright edge. For each helper H
touching d, its dark H--B row now gives a restricted six-form chi_H for
d. Choose one as the d pivot. The other k-1 dark rows are differences
chi_H+chi_reference and belong to K_P(d), of rank at most one by F3.
All other non-EE residuals are in R10.

Any EE row with d and a touched bright b is already in this dark-row
span modulo R10. Indeed, if H uses pivot bright a, with corresponding
boundary endpoints B_a,B_b,B_d, its short bright row is

             L=psi_a+psi_b+B_a+B_b in R10.

For an EE equation d+b+ell=0, its residual is

   (chi_reference+chi_H)+L+(B_d+B_b+psi_ell).

The final term is itself a boundary dependency of weight at most six,
since ell is represented or bright. Hence it lies in R10.

EE rows without d are good. Rows d+represented+represented are short.
Rows d+bright+represented provide restricted six-forms for d and all
their quotient differences lie in K_P(d). Consequently at least TWO
EE rows using d and two UNTOUCHED bright colors are required outside
the at-most-one-dimensional K_P(d).

The k-1 dark H--B rows have rank at most min(k-1,1). Thus potentially
contributing EE rows number at least 3-min(k-1,1): three if k=1, two
if k>=2. For fixed nonzero d, the pairs (a,b) with a+b=d are disjoint:
one chosen color forces the other. Distinct EE equations cannot repeat
the same unordered triple, by defining-equation independence.

Every contributing EE row consumes at least one untouched bright
color; the two required bright--bright rows each consume one extra.
Therefore

                  qB-t >= max(4,6-k),       k>=1.         (6)

Together with t>=k this gives qB>=6, hence q>=7. For canonical reverses,
t>=2k strengthens it to q>=1+2k+max(4,6-k), in particular q>=8 if k>=1.

CASE k=0.

The dark label has no mixed defining or use edge at all: there are no
H--H edges, no B--B reference, and any anchored H--B contact would make
it bright. It is EE-born and all its uses are at EE gates. Pivot d by
one EE equation whose other pair uses as few bright colors as possible.
All non-EE rows and all EE rows without d are in R10. At least four EE
triangles containing d are necessary, since one is consumed as d's pivot.

  * A represented--represented pivot gives d a four-form. Only EE
    bright--bright pairs can contribute. Three disjoint such pairs
    require at least six bright colors.

  * With a bright--represented pivot and no represented--represented
    pair anywhere, all bright--represented forms are restricted.
    Their differences have rank at most one by F3, so at least two
    bright--bright pairs are needed. Four pairs, each consuming at
    least one bright color and at least two consuming two, require
    at least six distinct bright colors.

  * If all pairs are bright--bright, four triangles require eight
    bright colors.

Thus qB>=6 and q>=7 also for k=0. This proves the one-dark theorem.

6. AGGREGATE CONSEQUENCES

Let A=sum_(r=3)u. The inherited full-helper incompatibility says each
missing color meets at most one full helper. Thus A equals the number
of full-bright missing colors and in particular uses A distinct
repeated-bright colors.

Zero exceptions are impossible when z=0, giving A<=q-1. If q<=6,
one weak bright exception is impossible by section 4 and one dark
exception by section 5. At least two exceptions are needed, so

          A<=q-2,       when z=0 and q<=6.                (7)

No canonical-reverse hypothesis is required for this cut; liveness
and normalization suffice.

The same exceptional-color count yields an additional capacity cut. Define

  Z=sum_i (r_i-1)_+ u_i,       E=sum_(r_i=3) max(u_i-2,0).

For each fixed missing color e, the inherited weighted capacity theorem
gives Z_e<=3. See research_next/dual/SUPPLEMENT.md section 3 in the 75
source bundle: its complete projected-row independence check covers all
616 repeated-helper values and all choices of their representations.
An exceptional color has Z_e=0 by definition. If p is the number of
repeated-bright colors, this already gives Z<=3p.

The stronger inherited overflow argument also restricts E to this same
set of p colors. The complete high-excess set consists of the 192
transpose colors capable of Z_e=3. Every actual full helper meets at
most two such colors. Thus each full helper has at least (u_i-2)_+
incidences at low-excess colors, all repeated-bright. These charged
colors are distinct across full helpers. If L_P counts the selected
repeated-bright colors outside the high-excess set, then

                     Z<=3p-L_P,        E<=L_P.

Consequently Z+E<=3p. This is precisely the proof in
research_deep/helpers/HIGH_EXCESS_CAPACITY.md restricted to the colors
with positive repeated-helper contact; exceptional colors cannot pay
for E and are disjoint from its charge. Therefore

  z=0                ==> Z+E<=3(q-1),
  z=0 and q<=6       ==> Z+E<=3(q-2).                      (7a)

The second line implies the simpler Z<=3(q-2). These rows use inherited
per-label capacities plus the new exceptional-color theorem, without a
new finite premise. Under the all-bright hypothesis, sections 4-5 force
at least two exceptions for EVERY q, so Z+E<=3(q-2) also holds there
without the q<=6 condition; this last form is explicitly conditional.

There is a further coupling valid for ARBITRARY z. Let

                   h0=sum_(r_i=0)1

count unanchored helpers, with coefficient one per such helper. If h0=0
and Q_* were empty, every helper would have a triple pivot and every
missing color a repeated-bright four-form pivot. Every H--H residual
would then have weight at most 3+3+4=10, every H--B residual at most
eight, and every EE residual would lie in R10 by F1. All nonboundary
variables would be eliminated and all 32 independent boundary rows
would lie in the rank-29 space R10, a contradiction. This argument
does not require z=0.

Therefore when h0=0, at least one exceptional label is necessary:
p<=q-1. Using A<=p and Z+E<=3p yields the general linear rows

                  A-h0 <= q-1,
              Z+E-3h0 <= 3(q-1).                         (7b)

For h0>=1, these rows are implied by the inherited A<=q and Z+E<=3q,
so they hold in all cases. They apply to M^T at arbitrary z and need
no maximal-y, canonical-reverse, or transpose-stabilization assumption.

7. ALL-BRIGHT CIRCUITS NEED THREE COLORS WITHOUT FULL CONTACTS

Assume z=0 and every missing label is bright. Suppose at most two
missing colors w1,w2 are not full-bright. All non-EE residuals are
short after the standard pivots and unanchored five-form pivots.

An EE row using a represented color is short. For an EE row using only
missing colors, classify its colors outside the complete palette P3:

  zero outside: it is good by F1;
  one outside: it belongs to that color's rank-one F2/P3 space;
  two outside: the pair forces the third color; it belongs to F4.

There cannot be three outside colors under the hypothesis. If an actual
non-full-bright color nevertheless lies in P3, this only reduces the
number of exceptions. By F4, the total EE quotient rank is at most two.
It cannot supply the required three dimensions. Thus at least three
missing colors have no full-helper contact, proving

          A<=q-3,       when z=0 and all missing colors bright.     (8)

This last theorem uses F4 and is conditional on ALL missing colors
being bright. It is not included in the scalar scan reported by the
current checkpoint, which does not track that dark-color hypothesis.
It must not be silently imposed when dark status is unspecified.

8. THE SAVED G=75 AGGREGATE POINT IS NOT AN ACTUAL CIRCUIT

The transpose side of SURVIVING_MODEL_POINT_75.json has x=25,y=3,h=15,
q=4,z=0, three bright colors, and one genuinely dark color. Its helpers:

  12 anchored helpers with (r,u)=(3,0);
   1 anchored helper with (r,u)=(3,1);
   1 anchored helper with (r,u)=(3,2);
   1 unanchored helper with TWO BRIGHT and ONE DARK incidence.

The last helper's total degree is three. Its six-field saved row must
not be read as total u=2: the separate dark-incidence field adds one.
The degree sum is 12*3+4+5+3=48, agreeing with 32+h-y+q=48.

All three bright colors touch full helpers. Hence there is exactly one
exceptional color, which is dark, but q=4 violates q>=7. Equivalently
A=3 violates (7), which requires A<=2 at q=4. The entire incidence
class is excluded regardless of gate count, masks, or depth.
