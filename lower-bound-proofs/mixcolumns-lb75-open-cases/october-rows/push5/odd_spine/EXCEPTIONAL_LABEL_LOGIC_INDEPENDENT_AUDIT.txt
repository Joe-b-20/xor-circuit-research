INDEPENDENT LOGIC AUDIT: EXCEPTIONAL EE LABELS
2026-10-22

AUDIT RESULT

The pivot, chronology, sharing, and quotient-capacity arguments described
below are sound. The finite capacities are separate premises: the root's
independent enumeration regenerates them. This note audits their use in
the proofs, not a second regeneration of those finite word tables.

The resulting necessary conditions concern arbitrary live normalized
circuits for M transpose, with no H--H edges (z=0):

  * zero exceptional missing labels is impossible;
  * exactly one exceptional label which is weak bright is impossible,
    for every number q of missing labels;
  * exactly one exceptional label which is dark requires q>=7;
  * therefore q<=6 requires at least two exceptional labels.

Two immediate aggregate consequences in the last case are

  A <= q-2,          Z <= 3(q-2),

where A=sum_(r=3)u and Z=sum_(r>=2)(r-1)u. Both require Mt,z=0,q<=6.

Here repeated-bright means a missing label with an actual H--B incidence
at a helper having r>=2 represented incidences; exceptional means a
missing label which is not repeated-bright.

A separate strengthened corollary, using the full-helper palette, is also
logically valid conditional on its stated finite joint capacity: with z=0
and NO dark missing labels, at least three missing labels fail to be
full-bright (incident at an r=3 helper). Thus A<=q-3, where the inherited
full-helper incompatibility identifies A=sum_(r=3)u with the number of
distinct full-bright labels.

1. ALL PIVOTS ARE LEGITIMATE ACTUAL EQUATIONS

Let R10 be the formal boundary relation space of weight at most ten,
with rank 29. The full circuit produces 32 independent boundary relations.

Pivot represented labels by their actual B--B reference rows. Pivot each
anchored helper by an actual represented H--B row. These give two- and
three-boundary forms respectively. Pivot bright missing labels by actual
H--B rows at anchored helpers, giving forms of size at most four. A
repeated-bright label can be pivoted at a helper with r>=2, ensuring its
four-form contains a repeated-helper triple whenever it has size four.

Every unanchored helper used below has a bright contact and can be pivoted
there, giving a form of size at most five. This requirement is automatic
in the stated live z=0 cases. A nonoutput odd helper has its defining odd
edge and at least one use edge, so its graph degree is at least two. If
there is only one dark label, label matching prevents both incidences from
having that label. An unanchored helper has no represented H--B incidence,
and z=0 excludes H--H edges. It must therefore touch a bright label.
When all missing labels are bright the conclusion is immediate.

All selected pivot rows are distinct actual gate equations, and each
eliminates its intended variable with coefficient one. After eliminating
every nonboundary variable, exactly 32 independent boundary rows remain.
No birth orientation is assumed: an EE equation is the unordered relation
among its result and its two parents. Pivoting a variable algebraically
does not assert that the corresponding gate physically produced it.

2. WHY CANCELLATION AND ALTERNATIVE FORMS ARE COVERED

Every two four-boundary forms for the same label differ by a dependency
of weight at most eight. Consequently they are identical modulo R10.
If a missing label has a conceivable boundary-pair form, it can be
replaced by that pair modulo R10, whether or not an actual B--B reference
exists. Any EE relation containing such a pair and two four-forms then
has weight at most ten and contributes no quotient direction.

Thus a nonzero EE quotient relation among three four-forms must be a
weight-twelve relation with a DISJOINT 4+4+4 split. Cancelling any repeated
boundary coordinate would shorten it to weight at most ten. The complete
4+4+4 census therefore covers all potentially nonzero EE rows, including
all alternate choices of pivots and all cancellations.

Similarly, a difference of two at-most-six forms can be nonzero modulo
R10 only if both forms have size six, are disjoint, and give one of the
complete weight-twelve 6+6 splits. These are exactly the cases used by the
restricted-six capacity audit.

3. ONE WEAK-BRIGHT EXCEPTION

Assume every missing label except w is repeated-bright, while w is bright
but not repeated-bright. There are no dark labels. All non-EE residual
boundary rows have weight at most ten. Every EE row without w is good and
lies in R10 by the all-repeated-bright finite fact. A row containing w and
a represented label also lies there, by the bound 4+4+2<=10.

All remaining quotient rows therefore have the form w+b1+b2 with two
repeated-bright labels. The complete 4+4+4 audit groups such rows by the
ACTUAL even value w and bounds their combined quotient span by dimension
two. That bound includes all possible four-forms for w and both b's. If
w itself happens to belong to the repeated-helper palette despite having
only weak actual contacts, the all-palette zero-capacity fact is stronger.

Consequently all 32 residual boundary relations span at most 29+2=31
dimensions, a contradiction. The conclusion is impossibility for every q;
the earlier q>=7 counting statement is superseded by this capacity bound.

4. RESTRICTED SIX-FORMS: EXACT COVERAGE OF THE ONE-DARK CASE

A restricted six-form means either any even boundary form of size at most
four, or a size-six form containing a triple whose evaluated helper value
has at least two boundary-triple representations. The new finite premise
is that differences of all restricted forms of any fixed actual even
value d have quotient rank at most ONE modulo R10. Call this space K_d.

Every dark H--B pivot form chi_H is restricted: pivot H by one repeated-
bright four-form psi_b plus its boundary endpoint, then use the dark
endpoint. If the resulting six-form has no cancellation, it contains the
chosen repeated-helper triple. If cancellation occurs, its size is at most
four and it is restricted by definition. The same argument covers every
d=bright+represented form. A represented+represented form has size at
most four and is automatically restricted.

Therefore the finite restricted-six census covers ALL of the forms used
in the argument. It is not enough to count only selected canonical forms;
the stated census instead includes every relevant six-form with the
required literal triple and all shorter forms, whose differences vanish
automatically when paired with an at-most-six form.

5. ONE DARK LABEL WITH k>=1 DARK-INCIDENT HELPERS

Let k count unanchored helpers incident to d; all its H--B contacts are
unanchored because d is dark. Let t count the distinct bright labels that
share one of these helpers with d. Distinct helpers cannot share both d
and the same bright label: equality of their two boundary differences
violates Sidonicity or gives a four-row gate-equation dependency. Thus
the touched bright sets are disjoint. Liveness gives t>=k; canonical
reverse degree three would give the stronger t>=2k, but is not needed.

Pivot d through one dark-incident helper. The other k-1 dark rows are
differences of restricted six-forms, so together have quotient rank at
most min(k-1,1). All other non-EE residuals lie in R10.

An EE row using d and a TOUCHED bright label adds no dimension beyond
these dark rows. Here is the exact cancellation. At a helper H, choose
the bright pivot a with endpoint A, the dark endpoint D, and the touched
bright b with endpoint B. Its local dark form is chi_H=psi_a+A+D; its
remaining bright row is psi_a+A+B+psi_b in R10. An EE row d+b+ell, after
adding the dark pivot-change row and that bright row, reduces to

  D+B+psi_ell.

This is itself a true boundary dependency of weight at most six, since
ell is represented or bright. Thus it lies in R10. This calculation is
valid for every EE birth orientation and every choice of helper pivot.

All EE rows d+bright+represented lie in K_d modulo the chosen dark pivot.
Only d+bright+bright rows with BOTH bright colors untouched can escape
K_d. Since K_d has dimension at most one and three quotient dimensions
are required, at least two such escaping EE rows are necessary.

For fixed nonzero d, the pairs (a,b) with a+b=d are disjoint. A repeated
unordered triple would repeat an actual defining equation, forbidden by
normalization and gate-equation independence. Hence the two required
bright--bright rows consume four distinct untouched colors. When k=1,
there is no non-EE dark row at all, so at least three potentially
contributing EE rows are needed; the third consumes another untouched
bright color. Therefore

  qB-t >= max(4,6-k),    k>=1.

Combining t>=k gives qB>=6 and hence q=qB+1>=7. Extra sharing of physical
signals or extra EE gates cannot evade the fixed-d pair disjointness.

6. THE k=0 CASE IS COVERED SEPARATELY

If d has no H--B contacts, z=0 and darkness also exclude all other odd
graph incidences. It must be EE-born and can be pivoted by an EE equation.
All non-EE boundary residuals lie in R10. At least four EE equations using
d are needed: one is consumed as its pivot and at least three residual
rows must supply the missing quotient rank.

Choose a d pivot minimizing its number of bright partners.

  RR exists. Give d a four-form. Every d row with a represented partner
  lies in R10. Three distinct bright--bright rows are required, consuming
  six distinct bright colors.

  No RR but BR exists. The pivot is restricted six. Differences of all
  bright--represented forms have quotient rank at most one. At least two
  bright--bright rows are needed to escape that space. There are at least
  four d equations total, and none is RR. Their disjoint partner pairs
  therefore consume at least 2+2+1+1=6 bright colors.

  All pairs BB. At least four disjoint pairs consume at least eight bright
  colors. No short-form capacity assumption is needed in this last case.

Thus qB>=6 also when k=0. This closes the possible EE-only dark-label gap.

7. TWO NON-FULL-BRIGHT EXCEPTIONS, WITH NO DARK LABELS

This corollary uses a different palette: full-bright colors have an actual
contact at an r=3 helper. Suppose z=0, every missing color is bright, and
at most two colors w1,w2 are not full-bright. The same actual pivots make
all non-EE rows lie in R10.

The additional complete finite premises are:

  (a) for fixed w, EE quotient rows w+fullbright+fullbright span at most
      one dimension;
  (b) for an unordered pair w1,w2 supporting a nonzero quotient triangle
      with one full-bright color, adjoin that direction to BOTH individual
      spaces in (a); their joint span has dimension at most one.

All EE rows are classified by their number of exceptional colors. Zero
exceptions gives a good row. One gives a direction in an individual space.
Two gives the actual triple w1+w2+s, whose third value s is forced. A
duplicate such triple cannot be an additional actual gate equation.
Three exceptions cannot occur because there are at most two colors.

If the two-exception triple has a nonzero quotient direction, (b) bounds
the complete EE quotient span by one. If it has no nonzero direction, the
two individual spaces together have dimension at most two. Either way
the required three-dimensional quotient cannot be supplied. Thus at least
three colors must be outside the full-bright set.

The final aggregate translation A<=q-3 uses the INHERITED separate fact
that a missing label contacts at most one full helper; that fact identifies
A=sum_(r=3)u with the count of distinct full-bright colors. The new
full-helper REFERENCE-label disjointness proved elsewhere concerns
represented labels and must not be substituted for this missing-label
incompatibility premise. That inherited fact was also independently
regenerated during this audit: all actual-compatible pairs of full helper
values have differences outside B+B. The complete M/Mt counts are in
FULL_HELPER_LABEL_OWNERSHIP_INDEPENDENT.json.

8. WEIGHTED EXCESS COROLLARY

For each missing color e, let

  z_e = sum_{helpers incident to e, r>=2}(r-1).

The inherited complete per-color weighted-capacity theorem gives z_e<=3.
Its per-color form is explicitly proved in Section 3 of
research_next/dual/SUPPLEMENT.md, using the rank-seven projected-row bound,
full-helper incompatibility, and the complete r3/r2/r2 exclusion.

An exceptional label has NO actual anchored H--B incidence at an r>=2
helper, by definition. It therefore has z_e=0. When Mt,z=0,q<=6, the
one-exception exclusions proved above require at least two exceptional
labels. At most q-2 missing labels can contribute to Z=sum_e z_e, giving

  Z <= 3(q-2).                                           (18)

Similarly, only repeated-bright labels can meet an r=3 helper. At most
q-2 labels can do so, and full-helper incompatibility limits each to one
such incidence. Hence

  A <= q-2.                                             (19)

No canonical-reverse hypothesis is used in these two conditional cuts.
For q<2 their negative right-hand sides express impossibility of the
entire z=0 class. The restriction q<=6 is essential for this particular
two-exception deduction; it must remain visible in a global relaxation.

9. LIMITATIONS OF THIS AUDIT

The finite facts used here are precisely the all-palette zero-capacity
fact, the fixed weak-label rank-two bound, the restricted-six rank-one
bound, and the full-bright individual/joint capacities. Their independent
enumeration is owned by the root audit. This note verifies that these
facts cover all formal residuals and that the resulting dimension and
color counts survive arbitrary sharing, cancellations, output reuse,
unbounded depth, and every EE result/parent orientation.

No unrestricted numerical gate lower bound is asserted by this note
alone. Its conditions must be integrated into a complete global budget
or certificate argument before a numerical improvement is claimed.
