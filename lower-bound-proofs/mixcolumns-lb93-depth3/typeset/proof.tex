{\large Joe Bachir}\par
{\sffamily\small\color{muted} Review edition \textbullet\ 23 October 2026}\par
\begin{statementbox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\color{accent} THE LOWER-BOUND STATEMENT}\par
\vspace{3pt}
{\Large $D_3(M)\ge 93$}\par
Every binary XOR circuit computing one 32-bit column of AES forward MixColumns, with inputs at depth zero and output depth at most three, needs at least 93 gates. Arbitrary sharing, fan-out, cancellation, and asymmetry are permitted.
\end{statementbox}
\textbf{Proof route.} A complete depth-three normal form reduces any hypothetical circuit with at most 92 gates to 26 pair helpers and 34 triple/quartet helpers. Parity leaves six, seven, or eight pair-graph components. Exact, complete checks exclude all three branches.\par
\textbf{Fresh verification.} The complete verification was rerun. It covered 275,354 six-component, 22,060 seven-component, and 110,712 eight-component partitions with zero uncovered cases. All 1,113 conditioned Farkas certificates passed, and the 97-gate construction was rechecked.\par
\textbf{Reading and reproduction.} Read the proof below, then use the package's \texttt{README.md} for the file map and environment details. From the extracted package folder, run:\par
\vspace{3pt}
{\ttfamily\small python3 verify\_all.py}\par
\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{Scope of the result.} The included construction gives $93\le D_3(M)\le97$. Gate counts 93 through 96 remain unresolved.\par
\textbf{Source and review status.} The full argument assembles the corrected 17 October 2026 proof chain. Original proof notes, the enumeration correction, exact certificates, and a checked 97-gate construction accompany it. The work was developed with AI assistance and is submitted for expert review. Exact computational checks are documented; the mathematical argument is not proof-assistant formalized.\par
