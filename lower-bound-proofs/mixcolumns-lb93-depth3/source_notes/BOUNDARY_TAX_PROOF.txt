An exact boundary-pair inequality for the weight-seven AES clusters
2026-10-16

MODEL AND STATUS

Gates are two-input XORs, inputs have depth zero, and output depth is at
most three. Fan-out and cancellation are unrestricted. This note proves
a local optimum and an exact decomposition of the twelve weight-seven
outputs. It does NOT prove the full 32-output 97-gate circuit optimal.
The resulting full-circuit interval is 86 <= D3 <= 97; see
FULL_BOUND_86.txt. The old 85 certificate remains valid separately.

1. THE FOUR-OUTPUT PROBLEM

Let a_0,...,a_3, b_0,...,b_3, c_0,...,c_3 be distinct inputs, indices modulo
four, and put

  Y_r = (a_0+a_1+a_2+a_3+a_r) + b_r+b_(r+1) + c_r+c_(r+1).

All sums in signal expressions are XOR. In counts, sums are ordinary
integer addition. A, B, C denote the three four-input families.

Every Y_r has weight seven. At depth three it must be the XOR of a
disjoint triple and quartet at depth two. The triple must be an input
plus a disjoint pair, and the quartet two disjoint pairs. Thus its
entire ancestry is cancellation-free: three pair occurrences, one
unpaired raw-input occurrence (the anchor), two parents, one output.

Delete unused gates and merge duplicate masks. Write P for the number of
distinct pairs and H for the number of distinct triple/quartet parents
in the four outputs' ancestry. Then G=4+P+H. A pair belongs to at most
two outputs, since the intersection of any three outputs has size one.
Consequently P>=6. Every triple/quartet also belongs to at most two.

Join the two parents of each output by an edge labelled by that output.
This is a simple bipartite graph (triples versus quartets) of maximum
degree two. It is a forest: XORing the edge labels on a cycle gives
zero, whereas no nonempty subset of these four outputs XORs to zero.
More specifically, subsets of sizes one, two, three, four have weights
7; 6 or 10; 5; and 4, respectively. Its components are therefore paths,
and 5<=H<=8.

2. EXACT LOCAL OPTIMUM: SIXTEEN GATES

If G<=15, the preceding bounds force P=6,H=5. The parent graph is a
four-edge path. For two vertices at distance d along it, their XOR is
the XOR of the d intervening output labels. The subset weights above
give the following intersection bounds:

  distance 1: intersection zero;
  distance 2 or 3: intersection at most one;
  distance 4: intersection one for triple endpoints,
              intersection two for quartet endpoints.

A pair can be used in two parents only if their intersection contains
both pair inputs. Therefore no construction pair can be shared except
possibly one pair between two quartet endpoints. A T-Q-T-Q-T path has
seven construction-pair slots and permits no such sharing. A Q-T-Q-T-Q
path has eight slots and permits at most one saving. Both need at least
seven distinct pairs, contradicting P=6. Hence G>=16.

The following circuit attains sixteen:

  depth 1 (six gates):
    p_r=b_r+c_r, for r=0,1,2,3;
    u=a_0+a_1; v=a_2+a_3.
  depth 2 (six gates):
    q_0=v+p_1; q_1=u+p_3;
    t_0=a_1+p_0; t_1=a_0+p_2;
    t_2=a_3+p_2; t_3=a_2+p_0.
  depth 3 (four gates):
    Y_0=q_0+t_0; Y_1=q_0+t_1;
    Y_2=q_1+t_2; Y_3=q_1+t_3.

3. BOUNDARY-PAIR THEOREM

Let k be the number of distinct pairs wholly within C that occur in
the four outputs' ancestry. Then

                      G >= 16+k.

Equivalently, with p=P-k, the claim is p+H>=12. The same claim holds
with B and C interchanged, but the two taxes cannot simply be added.

Only c_r+c_(r+1) can occur as C-pairs. Each belongs to exactly one
output and each output uses at most one. Thus 0<=k<=4. Counting the
twelve pair occurrences gives

                      2p+k >= 12.                 (1)

We need one structural fact. If an output uses a C-pair, a parent that
contains it is private, meaning it is contained in only this output.
Its companion parent is either private too, or is a triple with no C
input. In the latter case every quartet adjacent to this shared triple
is private, because it contains its output's entire C-pair. Hence an
output using a C-pair lies in a parent-graph component of at most two
edges.

Here is a direct check of the structural fact. If the C-pair is in the
triple, its third input is either an A input, leaving the whole B-pair
in the quartet, or a B input, leaving all three A inputs in the quartet.
Both leftovers are private. If the C-pair is in the quartet, its other
two inputs are AA, BB, or AB. In the first two cases the companion
triple contains a whole B-pair or all three A inputs, so is private.
In the AB case the companion triple has type AAB and contains no C.
Every other output using it has a quartet containing its whole C-pair,
which is private. This also proves the fact for any actual choice of
pair constructions; no assumption about preferred pairings is made.

Now exhaust the four possible values of H.

H=5. There is one four-edge path. The structural fact forces k=0.
Section 2 then gives p=P>=7, hence p+H>=12.

H=8. Equation (1) and k<=4 give p>=4, hence p+H>=12.

H=7. If k<=3, (1) gives p>=5. The only remaining possibility violating
the claim is k=4,p=4. Every output then uses its C-pair and two pairs
within A union B. All four non-C pairs must occur in exactly two
outputs. For an A or B input v, let d_v be its degree in the graph of
these four non-C pairs and let a_v count its anchor occurrences. Then

  3=2*d_v+a_v for v in A;  2=2*d_v+a_v for v in B.

There are only four anchors in total. Each of the four A inputs needs
an odd, positive number of anchors, so each has exactly one; the B
inputs have none. Therefore all eight d_v equal one: the non-C pairs
form a perfect matching of A union B.

Every triple is now private. A triple containing C-pair is private
already; any other triple can contain at most one edge of this matching
and therefore has a uniquely determined anchor. The four outputs have
different anchors, so they cannot share a triple. Every quartet is
also private: if it contains the C-pair this is immediate, and if not,
its companion triple contains the C-pair, so the structural fact makes
the quartet private. All eight parents are private, contradicting H=7.

H=6. There are two path components, with edge counts 1+3 or 2+2.
In the 1+3 case, only the single-edge component can use a C-pair, so
k<=1 and (1) gives p>=6. For 2+2, k=0 is immediate from (1).

For 2+2 and k>0, choose a component containing a C-pair. Its shared
parent is a triple with no C input. Outputs sharing any parent must
be adjacent: opposite outputs intersect in only two inputs. A rotation
or reflection therefore lets us label this component's outputs 0,1.
Their intersection is {a_2,a_3,b_1,c_1}. The shared triple must be

  t=a_2+a_3+b_1,

and their two quartets are Y_0+t and Y_1+t. The remaining outputs 2,3
intersect in {a_0,a_1,b_3,c_3}. Their common parent is either that
quartet or one of its four triples. These are all five possibilities.

Each of the six parents has exactly three constructions from pairs
(three choices of the anchor for a triple; three perfect matchings
for a quartet). Thus only 5*3^6=3645 assignments remain. The exact
minimum number p of non-C pairs, grouped by k>0, is:

  second component's common parent     k=1  k=2  k=3  k=4
  {a_0,a_1,b_3,c_3}                       7    7    -    -
  triple omitting a_0                     7    7    -    -
  triple omitting a_1                     7    7    -    -
  triple omitting b_3                     8    8    -    -
  triple omitting c_3                     8    7    7    6

Here '-' means no such assignment. Every entry is at least six, giving
p+H>=12. The included standard-library verifier enumerates exactly
these assignments using integer masks, without any optimization solver.
Together with the other three cases this proves G>=16+k.

4. EXACT SEPARATION OF AES CLUSTER 1

Write F_b={x_(8r+b): r=0,1,2,3}. AES forward MixColumns has precisely
the form above for its four outputs at bit b in {1,3,4}, with A=F_b,
B=F_(b-1), C=F_7. Let D3(S) denote the minimum number of gates for
the output clusters with bit positions in S only.

Then

                 D3({1,3,4}) = 16 + D3({3,4}).

For the lower bound, take any circuit for these twelve outputs, and
let A1 and A34 be the sets of gates in the respective output ancestries.
Their input-support unions are F_0 union F_1 union F_7 and
F_2 union F_3 union F_4 union F_7. Cancellation-free ancestry forces
every shared gate's support to lie wholly in F_7. Since each output
has only two F_7 inputs, such a shared gate can only be a pair.

Let s=|A1 intersection A34| and let k count all F_7 pair masks in A1.
After merging duplicate masks, s<=k, so the boundary-pair theorem gives

  |A1 union A34| = |A1|+|A34|-s
                >= (16+k)+D3({3,4})-s
                >= 16+D3({3,4}).

Conversely, compose any optimum circuit for clusters 3,4 with the
sixteen-gate circuit in section 2 for cluster 1. This proves the
opposite inequality and hence equality. The explicit local circuit
uses no pure F_7 pair, so its gates cannot overlap an ancestry for
clusters 3,4, although disjoint composition alone suffices for the
upper bound.

5. LIMITS AND REPRODUCTION

The included 32-gate circuit for clusters 3,4 proves D3({3,4})<=32.
WEIGHTED_CLUSTER_PROOF.txt now proves the matching lower bound by
exact rational charging and complete enumeration. Thus D3({3,4})=32
and the 48-gate twelve-output construction is optimal. This separate
weighted proof is necessary; the boundary tax alone does not prove it.

The tax cannot be charged once for B and again for C: a valid circuit
with two A-pairs, four B-pairs, four C-pairs, four triples, four
quartets, and four outputs has 22 gates, while 16+k_B+k_C=24.

Run, from this directory:
  python verify_boundary_tax.py
  python verify_cluster_results.py

Both use only Python's standard library. The first checks the finite
table and structural support facts. The second independently derives
the AES matrix, checks the local sixteen-gate construction, verifies
the support-separation premise, and checks the saved partial circuits.
The complete argument combines these finite checks with the reasoning
above; the verifier's PASS is not claimed to formalize every prose step.
