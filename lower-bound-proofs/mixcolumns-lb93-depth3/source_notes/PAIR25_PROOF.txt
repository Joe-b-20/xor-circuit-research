Depth-three AES MixColumns requires at least 25 pair gates
2026-10-16

MODEL AND CLAIM

Inputs have depth zero, and two-input XOR gates have depth one plus the
maximum depth of their parents. Output depth is at most three. Sharing,
fan-out, and cancellation are unrestricted. Use the normalization proved in
PROOF.md: distinct depth-one pairs, depth-two triples and quartets, and 32
depth-three output gates. If P counts pairs, then

                                P >= 25.

This strengthens the earlier parity bound P>=24. It does not claim that 25
pairs suffice, or by itself determine the minimum full gate count.

1. THE PAIR GRAPH AND ITS PARITY PARTITION

Form a graph on the 32 input bits, with an edge for every pair signal.
Every odd signal in the normalized circuit is one raw input (its anchor)
plus a sum of pair signals. In each connected component the pair signals
have even support. Therefore each output intersects exactly one component
oddly and all other components evenly.

For the indicator u of a component, M*u is the indicator of the outputs
that intersect it oddly, where M is the AES binary matrix. The vectors

                                (u,M*u)

of the components have disjoint supports and partition all 64 input/output
coordinates. The previous parity-partition proof in PROOF.md, independently
checked by verify_pair_rank.py, excludes nine components. Thus c<=8.

Suppose P=24. A graph with 32 vertices and 24 edges has at least eight
components. Hence c=8. Its cycle rank is P-32+c=0, so every component is a
tree, including isolated vertices. The eight graph-code words above must
therefore be compatible with a forest on their input coordinates.

2. A FORCED EDGE FROM EACH TWO-INPUT INTERSECTION

At depth three, a weight-seven output must be a disjoint triple plus a
quartet. Its entire unfolded ancestry is cancellation-free: three disjoint
pair edges and one unpaired raw-input anchor cover its seven inputs.

Let C be any pair-graph component, and let Y be a weight-seven output.
If |C intersect supp(Y)|=2, those two inputs MUST be joined by a pair edge.

To prove this, the intersection has even size, so C cannot contain Y's
anchor: precisely the anchor's component has odd intersection with Y.
Both inputs must instead be covered by pair edges. A pair edge cannot leave
C, and no input outside supp(Y) can occur anywhere in this cancellation-free
ancestry. The only available partner for either input in C is the other one.

For any prospective component indicator u, define the forced-edge graph

  F(u) = { u intersect supp(Y) : wt(Y)=7 and wt(u intersect supp(Y))=2 }.

Every edge in F(u) must occur in the actual pair graph. If P=24, F(u) must
be a forest for every component. This deduction places no restriction on
weight-five output cancellation, which remains allowed.

3. THE FINITE EXCLUSION

There is no partition of the 64 coordinates into eight nonzero graph-code
words (u,M*u) for which every F(u) is a forest. The following is a complete
finite verification of that statement, implemented in verify_pair25.py.

Every graph-code word has even weight, because every column of M has odd
weight. The minimum nonzero weight is six, as checked by enumeration. In an
eight-word partition sorted by weight, the sixth word has weight at most
ten: if it had weight at least twelve, the total weight would be at least
5*6+3*12=66, exceeding 64. Thus the first six words have weight at most ten.

All words of weight at most ten can be generated by enumerating input
halves of weight at most five and output halves of weight at most five,
using M and its inverse. At least one half of such a word has weight at
most five, so this enumeration is complete. The counts are:

  total weight       all words       words whose F(u) is a forest
       6                 84                         72
       8                276                        194
      10               1300                        868

The checker enumerates every sorted disjoint choice of six forest-compatible
short words, pruning only by disjointness, canonical order, and the remaining
total weight. Let R be the complement of such a six-word union. Since the
all-ones vector is a graph-code word, R is also a graph-code word.

Write I,O for R's input and output supports. All graph-code words supported
inside R are obtained by solving the exact homogeneous binary system

               M[rows outside O, columns in I] * v = 0.

The checker computes a basis for this kernel by binary row reduction,
enumerates every vector in it, and pairs each proper nonzero word w with
R XOR w. It tests the forced-edge forests of both words and their order
after the first six words. This enumerates every possible final two blocks;
there is no assumption that their weights are at most ten.

The independent standard-library check returns:

  search nodes:                      2,554,272
  six-word leaves:                     399,570
  distinct remaining supports:          36,716
  compatible eight-block partitions:         0.

Consequently P=24 is impossible. Together with P>=24, this proves P>=25.

4. INDEPENDENT DISCOVERY CROSS-CHECK AND LIMITS

A separate C++ enumeration first generated all 3,067,503 eight-block parity
partitions, including components whose forced-edge graphs contain cycles.
A second pass found a forced cycle in every partition. The compact Python
checker derives its own short words and performs its own kernel enumeration;
it does not read that partition list or trust its count.

The sum of the forced-edge cycle ranks can be as small as one in an
eight-block parity partition. Thus simply replacing 'a cycle' by 'two cycles'
would be false. The present argument does not establish P>=26.

REPRODUCE

  python verify_pair_rank.py
  python verify_pair25.py

Both require only Python's standard library. The first checks the earlier
c<=8 premise. The second checks the new finite exclusion, in a few seconds
on the development machine. The proof combines the mathematical reductions
above with the exact finite checks; it is not a proof-assistant formalization.
