Exact depth-three complexity of the twelve weight-seven AES outputs: 48
2026-10-16

This is a computer-assisted exact proof for a subsystem of MixColumns.
It allows arbitrary two-input XOR gates, sharing, and cancellation at
depth at most three. The full 32-output optimum remains unresolved.

1. WHAT IS BEING COUNTED

Let C_b be the four forward MixColumns outputs at bit position b, using
least-significant-bit-first indexing. The weight-seven outputs are
exactly C_1, C_3, C_4. Let D3(S) count the minimum gates computing the
clusters in S only. Inputs are at depth zero, gates have fan-in two,
and fan-out is unrestricted.

BOUNDARY_TAX_PROOF.txt proves, without restricting the circuit topology,

  D3({b})=16, b=1,3,4;
  D3({1,3,4})=16+D3({3,4}).

We now prove D3({3,4})=32, hence D3({1,3,4})=48.

2. THE COMPLETE SHARED-HELPER LIST

For this section write A_i=x_(8i+3), C_i=x_(8i+7), indices modulo four.
These are the two input families common to clusters 3 and 4.

A weight-seven output's depth-three ancestry is cancellation-free and
contains exactly three pair occurrences, a triple, a quartet, and the
output. This follows from the weight argument in BOUNDARY_TAX_PROOF.txt;
it is a consequence of depth and parity, not an imposed restriction.

Enumerating all possible parents and their three pair constructions
gives exactly the following 28 possible common helpers. There are four
of each type and no common quartets. A signal is represented by the
set of inputs XORed together.

  helper type                         charge to C_3  charge to C_4
  A_i+A_(i+1)                             9/10          1/10
  A_(i+1)+C_i                             8/10          2/10
  A_i+C_i                                 1/10          9/10
  A_i+C_(i+1)                             8/10          2/10
  C_i+C_(i+1)                             1/10          9/10
  A_i+A_(i+1)+C_i                         2/10          8/10
  A_(i+1)+C_i+C_(i+1)                     8/10          2/10

Every other possible helper is charged one to its own cluster. The
checker independently regenerates both complete helper universes and
checks that their intersection is precisely this 28-element list.

3. THE TWO FINITE WEIGHTED INEQUALITIES

For every circuit computing C_3 alone, the charged cost of its helpers
is at least 115/10. For every circuit computing C_4 alone, it is at
least 116/10. Outputs are not included in these two helper costs.

These are exact finite inequalities, checked as follows. For each
seven-input output:

  - choose its triple parent in binomial(7,3)=35 ways;
  - its complementary quartet is determined;
  - choose the triple's construction in three ways;
  - choose the quartet's construction in three ways.

There are exactly 315 distinct recipes. A recipe is the set of the
five helper masks used by that complete output construction. A circuit
on four outputs chooses one recipe for each output, so all possibilities
occur among 315^4=9,845,600,625 recipe tuples. A shared helper is charged
only once, by taking the union of the four recipes.

The standard-library Python generator and small C++ enumerator in
weighted_cluster_certificate.py and exact_weighted_enum.cpp enumerate
this complete space with nonnegative-cost pruning. All charges are
multiplied by ten and all cost arithmetic is integer arithmetic.

The two permitted prunings are elementary:
  (a) a partial union cannot become cheaper when another recipe is added;
  (b) the extra cost of any complete extension is at least the minimum
      extra cost of satisfying any one remaining output.

The enumerator keeps an upper bound from an actual complete tuple and
discards a branch only when one of these lower bounds is at least that
upper bound. Recipe sorting changes the visitation order only. The
checker requires the enumerator to report COMPLETE; a timeout would
not certify anything. It also verifies an attaining tuple for each
minimum directly against the regenerated recipes and integer weights.

The independently regenerated exact results are:

  C_3: minimum weighted helper numerator 115;
       5,493,600 recursion candidates, 113,206,275 final-output candidates.
  C_4: minimum weighted helper numerator 116;
       7,137,585 recursion candidates, 235,201,050 final-output candidates.

These are complete enumeration results, not numerical optimizer bounds.
Different recipes can suggest different constructions of the same
parent mask. This does not invalidate completeness: a real circuit is
certainly represented, and conversely any union supplies at least one
valid depth-two construction for each selected parent. Choose any such
construction and reuse its result wherever required.

4. COMBINE THE CHARGES AND ROUND

Take any joint circuit and restrict it to the ancestries of C_3 and
C_4. Normalize duplicate masks away, which never increases gate count
or depth. A helper used by both clusters receives complementary charges
whose sum is one. A helper used by only one cluster receives a charge
at most one. Therefore the total actual number of helpers is at least
the sum of the two charged costs:

  helpers >= 115/10+116/10=231/10.

There are eight distinct output gates, none reusable as an ancestor at
depth three. Consequently

  G >= 8+231/10=311/10=31.1.

Because G is an integer, G>=32. The included cluster34_circuit.json
has exactly 32 gates, independently verified at depth three. Therefore

                       D3({3,4})=32.

By the boundary-pair separation theorem,

                       D3({1,3,4})=16+32=48.

The included cluster134_circuit.json attains 48. Alternatively compose
three copies of the explicit sixteen-gate construction. All these
claims count actual XOR gates, not formulas, leaves, or parent slots.

5. AN ADDITIONAL CLASSIFICATION CHECK

verify_local_optima.py enumerates all optimal helper-mask sets for one
four-output cluster. P>=6, H>=5 and P+H=12 leave only (P,H)=(6,6) or
(7,5), so only 322 parent sets and 232,794 construction assignments need
be inspected. There are 126 optimal helper sets: 18 of type (P,T,Q)
=(6,4,2), and 108 of type (7,2,3). No optimal helper set for C_3 overlaps
any optimal helper set for C_4 (15,876 pairs checked).

This counts helper-mask sets, not wire-level realizations. It explains
why composing two local optima gives no sharing, but it is not used as
a substitute for the weighted proof: nonoptimal local ancestries can
share more helpers, and the weighted proof includes all of them.
