AES forward MixColumns: a conditional depth-three lower bound
2026-10-17

THEOREM

For every normalized two-input XOR circuit computing forward MixColumns
at depth at most three, if P=26 and its pair graph has c=8 connected
components, then T+Q>=38. Hence its gate count is at least

                       G=32+26+38=96.

P,T,Q count distinct pair, triple, and quartet helpers in the lossless
normal form. Fan-out, sharing, and cancellation are unrestricted. No
symmetry is imposed on the circuit. The theorem is conditional: it does
not exclude six- or seven-component 26-pair circuits.

1. ODD/EVEN PARITY IDENTIFIES THE FINITE PARTITION CASES

Every output has odd weight, and its unfolded depth-three ancestry has one
raw-input anchor plus pair signals. Pair edges lie inside graph components.
Thus each output intersects exactly one pair component oddly. If M is the
32-by-32 forward MixColumns binary matrix, each component indicator u gives
a graph-code word (u,M*u). These words partition all 64 coordinates.

The independent row computation verifies that M has rank 32 and all row
and column parities are odd. Every nonzero graph-code word has even weight
at least six, by the previous verified graph-code lemma.

For P=26,c=8 the graph cycle rank is beta=P-32+c=2. For each component C,
weight-seven outputs meeting C in exactly two bits force the pair edge
joining those bits. The cycle rank of this forced graph is a lower bound
on the component cycle rank. A previously certified local forest
obstruction gives another lower bound of one. Taking the maximum of these
bounds per component, then adding over disjoint components, is valid.

Enumerate every eight-word partition subject to total lower-bound tax <=2.
The first six sorted words have weight at most ten, because otherwise
5*6+3*12>64. All short words are generated from M and its inverse; all
splits of the last remaining support are found by exact binary kernels.
There are 110,712 partitions after this necessary tax filter. The corrected
Python and C++ enumerators agree on the full set.

For 109,160 partitions, no pair-recipe cover with cycle rank <=2 exists.
The exact checker considers all 105 minimal pair recipes for each of the
12 weight-seven outputs and all 825 for each of the 20 weight-five outputs.
The latter include every possible foreign cancellation bit. Branching
covers every viable recipe of a chosen unsatisfied output. Forced edges
and cycle-rank monotonicity give safe pruning. All cases finish; no timeout
or UNKNOWN is interpreted as infeasibility.

The remaining 1,552 partitions form 388 orbits under cyclic byte rotation.
The actual matrix symmetry is verified before rotating certificates. This
only reuses conditional proofs; it does not restrict circuit symmetry.

2. MAKE EVERY INTERNAL PAIR FREE

Fix one remaining component partition. Give the circuit all possible pairs
whose endpoints lie in a common component, at zero cost. This can only
make constructing the final parents easier.

A normalized parent has weight at most four. Its availability in this
relaxation is determined exactly by intersections with the components:

  weight 1: always available;
  weight 2: its two bits lie in one component;
  weight 3: at least two of its bits lie in one component;
  weight 4: its intersection with every component has even size.

For weight three, use the internal pair and the remaining raw input. For
weight four, use two disjoint internal pairs; the intersection patterns
are either four bits in one component or two in each of two components.
The converses follow because a depth-two triple is pair+input and a
quartet is the sum of two disjoint pairs. These tests retain every actual
parent and permit extra ones.

For each output y, list all parent edges (a,b) with a XOR b=y, where a has
weight one or three and b has weight two or four, and both pass the tests.
Before filtering there are exactly 6,120 edges across the 32 outputs.
This list is regenerated independently of the discovery recipe generator.

3. THE ACTUAL OUTPUT-PARENT GRAPH IS A FOREST

Its vertices are distinct final-parent signals; each output contributes
one edge joining its actual parents and labeled by that output. Each label
appears once. If there were a cycle, XORing its edge labels would cancel
every vertex and give zero. That would be a nonempty linear dependence
among distinct output rows. M has rank 32, so no such cycle exists.

Thus the graph is a disjoint union of trees with distinct output labels.
Assign vertex cost zero to raw inputs and pairs, and one to triples and
quartets. Its total paid-vertex cost is at most T+Q.

4. EXACT TREE PRICES

For each of the 388 representative partitions the certificate gives signed
rational output prices lambda_0,...,lambda_31. The independent checker
regenerates all legal parent edges and enumerates every connected tree
whose edge labels are distinct. It checks the exact integer inequality

       sum(lambda_o over labels of tree F)
          <= number of triple/quartet vertices in F.             (1)

Prices are stored as integers over a positive common denominator. All
checking is integer arithmetic; numerical LP results are not trusted.
The independent enumeration grows rooted trees by every boundary edge with
a fresh output label. For graph components of at most 12 vertices a second
brute-force vertex-subset check verifies the same tree list.

Summing (1) over the actual output-parent forest counts each output label
exactly once, and each paid parent vertex at most once. Consequently

                   T+Q >= sum_o lambda_o.

The smallest exact certificate sum among all 388 representatives is 75/2.
The checker verifies 1,404,228 trees with multiplicity across all
representatives, using 2,459 distinct cached component graphs. Therefore

                       T+Q >= 75/2,

and integrality gives T+Q>=38. Together with the exact pair exclusions,
this covers every possible P=26,c=8 circuit and proves the theorem.

5. WHAT THIS REMOVES FROM THE 92-GATE CASE

The previously verified exact inequality 3P+4T+4Q>=214, together with P>=26,
forces every 92-gate normalized circuit to have P=26,T+Q=34. The established
component bound c<=8 then leaves (c,beta)=(6,0),(7,1),(8,2).
The new theorem rules out (8,2), because 34<38. The remaining cases are

                       (c,beta)=(6,0) or (7,1).

They have not been excluded. The verified unrestricted interval is still
92<=D3<=97. A 96-gate circuit in the eight-component branch is also not
excluded by this theorem.
