# Independent review: supplier differences and the first new clean direction

Hand review of `../SUPPLY_PROFILE.md` and its application to the specific producer in `../EXPORTS.md`. No SAT, enumeration, exact-gate search, or computer-driven case elimination was used. This is not formal verification.

## Verdict

The fixed-supplier one-coset deficiency certificate, the chronological span lemma, the reduction of the full `(11,10)` first/second-sheet profile to an actual augmented eleven-gate supplier, and the fixed two-diagonal producer's 22-gate application are correct under their stated hypotheses. In particular, the fixed-producer exclusion allows an additional zero-consumer-block gate that is produced by an exit; it does not incorrectly assume that all such gates have source-only ancestry. Neither the general joint 21-to-22 gap nor the augmented 11-versus-12 problem is resolved.

## Fixed-supplier certificate

For actual entry-label span `U`, the target free parts force `D subset U`. The occurrence-deletion/projection bound gives `K>=L_D+dim(U/D)`. If a hypothetical consumer has at most `L_D+1` gates, this quotient has dimension at most one. In dimension zero every entry is a clean member of the fixed availability set, so `D subset W`, contradicting `W!=D`.

In dimension one every nonclean entry belongs to the same nonzero `D`-coset. Choose one actual nonclean label `h`; any even-cardinality sum of nonclean labels is generated by pairwise differences with `h`, and adding an odd number remains outside `D`. Hence `U intersect D` is generated by actual clean entries and within-that-coset differences. It lies in `W+Delta_C`; because the target components span `D`, the assumed proper containment gives the contradiction. The anchor merely translates generators, so `Delta_C` is anchor-independent. The proof treats these differences as vector-space directions, not as physically produced gates. The hypothesis that no additional zero-`X` gates are being supplied for free is essential.

## Causal span and `(11,10)` reduction

Before the first extra zero-`X` gate whose value is clean and outside `W`, every actual entry used by a nonzero-`X` gate is in `W`: the hypothesis makes the entry clean, and by definition no earlier such outside value has appeared. Induction over gate order then puts the free part of every earlier nonzero-`X` signal in `W`. Ordinary `X` coordinate inputs have free part zero; an entry adds a `W` value; and a gate with two nonzero-`X` operands adds two existing `W` parts. If a zero-`X` gate reads nonzero-`X` operands, it must read two with equal nonzero `X` masks, and the sum of their free parts remains in `W`. A single nonzero-`X` operand and one zero-`X` operand cannot yield zero `X` part. Thus the first clean supplier outside `W` must have two zero-`X` operands.

In the specified joint profile, the first-sheet output cones `C0` have 10 or 11 gates by the one-sheet lower bound and the total zero-consumer-block count. If `|C0|=11`, it exhausts that count; every nonzero clean diagonal entry supplied to the ten-gate consumer is therefore a value of this source-only circuit. If `|C0|=10` and its clean span is deficient, the unique other zero-consumer-block gate must supply a new clean value, since the tight consumer's actual clean entries span all three dimensions. The causal lemma excludes its being an exit from two consumer-bearing operands. With no other zero-consumer-block gate outside `C0`, its zero-block operands are among the `C0` nodes and source-side inputs/free values, so appending it produces an actual eleven-gate source-only circuit containing the four first-sheet rows and three independent diagonals. If `C0` already has rank three, it is itself an augmented supplier. Therefore an augmented lower bound of 12 would exclude the full `(11,10)` profile, including feedback; the causal reduction does not establish that lower bound by itself.

## Fixed two-diagonal producer and additional-gate caveat

The explicit producer in EXPORTS has clean span `W=span(D01,D23)`. Its defect table and own-part parity show that any two of its available values in the same `D`-coset differ by a value in `W`, even after including ordinary source inputs and arbitrary free `a`-only vectors. Thus the fixed-supplier certificate rules out 11 or fewer consumer gates when no other zero-consumer-block gate is added.

At total cost 21, the only remaining split is one additional zero-consumer-block gate and ten consumer-bearing gates. All actual entries of that tight consumer are clean. Prior to the extra gate, induction places all consumer-bearing free parts in `W`. If the extra gate reads two original zero-block values and yields a clean value, that value is either a sum of two clean values in `W` or a difference of two values in one nonclean coset, also in `W`. If it reads two consumer-bearing values and cancels their consumer parts, the causal lemma puts its result in `W`. A nonclean result cannot be an entry of this ten-gate consumer. Hence the extra gate cannot supply the third clean direction and the 21-gate fixed-producer circuit is impossible even with such an exit. Two or more extra zero-block gates already make the total at least 22. The 22-gate construction in EXPORTS supplies a matching upper bound.

This is a statement about the **specified** ten-gate producer and exact physical accounting. The fixed-supplier deficiency test alone does not permit adding arbitrary further zero-block computations for free. The argument also does not generalize automatically to all ten-gate producers or to the other joint profile.
