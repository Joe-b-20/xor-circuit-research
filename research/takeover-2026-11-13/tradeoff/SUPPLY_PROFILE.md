# Supplier differences and the first new clean direction

2026-11-13. Hand arguments about actual supplied values and causal order. No circuit search or case enumeration. These results complement the [coset/clean-rank tradeoff](SUPPLY.md); they do not close the general joint 21-to-22 gap.

## A one-coset deficiency certificate

Let ordinary inputs span X and a disjoint free-side coordinate space be F. The required targets have nonzero X parts. Let D be the span of their F parts, and let L_D be their minimum cost when every value in D is free. Let Sigma be the values actually supplied on the F side. A consumer in this paragraph may read Sigma, but has no additional gates with zero X part. All its counted gates therefore have nonzero X part.

Define

    W = span(Sigma intersect D).

For each nonzero D-coset C meeting Sigma, choose a point h in Sigma intersect C and put

    Delta_C = span{v+h : v in Sigma intersect C}.

This definition is independent of the anchor h, and Delta_C is a subspace of D. If

    W != D, and W + Delta_C != D for every such C,

then every consumer has at least **L_D+2 gates**.

Indeed let U span its actual entry labels. The [entry-deletion bound](../compatibility/QUOTIENT.md) gives `K>=L_D+dim(U/D)`. Thus `K<=L_D+1` would force quotient dimension zero or one. In dimension zero, the targets force `D subset U subset W`, a contradiction. In dimension one, all nonclean actual labels lie in one coset C. The intersection of their span with D is generated by the actual clean labels and differences of nonclean labels from any one actual nonclean anchor. Therefore

    D = U intersect D is contained in W + Delta_C,

again a contradiction. The proof does not assign a physical gate to a virtual difference. It uses differences only to show that the actual entries cannot span the target free parts.

For four-triple sheets `L_D=10`, so this certificate forces at least twelve consumer gates. It can apply even when the supplier has two independent clean values. It is a test of a fixed supplied value set, and does not automatically permit extra zero-X computations at no cost.

## Feedback cannot create the first new clean direction

Suppose every entry label actually used by the nonzero-X portion of a circuit lies in D. Let W be a subspace of D containing all the clean labels available initially. Additional zero-X gates may occur during the computation.

**Causal span lemma.** The first zero-X gate with value in `D outside W`, if one appears before any such label has been supplied initially, cannot be the sum of two nonzero-X values. It must read two zero-X operands.

Before this first gate, every actual entry label lies in W. Induction puts the F part of every nonzero-X node in W: ordinary X inputs have zero F part; an entry adds a W label; a gate with two nonzero-X operands adds two such parts. If two earlier nonzero-X operands cancel their X parts, their resulting F value is still in W. A gate with exactly one nonzero-X operand cannot have zero X part. The only remaining possibility is two zero-X operands. This argument is chronological and allows arbitrary internal cancellations.

Thus an exit can produce a new physical supplier value, but cannot manufacture a new independent clean direction from a consumer that has so far read only the old clean span. It may still produce useful combinations within that span.

## The (11,10) joint profile reduces to an actual augmented supplier

Consider the joint 0/1 problem from [JOINT01](../row-cost/JOINT01.md): block a is free, ordinary blocks are b and b', and no ancestor of a designated first-sheet output has nonzero b' part. Suppose exactly eleven gates are zero on b' and ten are nonzero on b'. Wholly free a-only gates have been removed.

The tight ten-gate consumer theorem says that every actual entry label of the b'-bearing portion belongs to the clean even diagonal D on b+a, and those labels span D. This statement concerns actual operand values, including any values produced by feedback.

Let C0 be the union of the first-sheet target cones. It has ten or eleven gates, all zero on b' by hypothesis. If it has eleven, all suppliers are in this source-only circuit, which therefore already contains three independent diagonal values.

If C0 has ten gates, there is only one additional b'-zero gate g. Let W span the clean diagonal values available from C0. If W already has rank three, C0 itself is the required augmented supplier. Otherwise g must be a clean value outside W, since the consumer's actual clean entries span D and there is no other new supplier gate. Before g all clean entries lie in W. The causal span lemma shows that g cannot be an exit from two b'-bearing operands. Its two operands are b'-zero values already available from C0, ordinary b inputs, or the free a block. Appending g therefore gives an eleven-gate source-only circuit supplying the four first-sheet rows and three independent diagonals.

Consequently, a lower bound of twelve for the augmented source problem would exclude the entire (11,10) profile even when a putative joint circuit was allowed to contain a b'-cancelling exit. This fills the causal step implicit in the earlier reduction; it does not prove the augmented lower bound twelve.

## Applying the certificate to the two-diagonal producer

The explicit ten-gate producer in [EXPORTS](EXPORTS.md) supplies exactly two independent clean diagonals D01 and D23. Put `W=span{D01,D23}`. Its actual value table proves the stronger property

    Delta_C is contained in W for every D-coset C.

Hence the fixed-supplier certificate excludes a consumer with eleven or fewer gates. It also excludes a twenty-one-gate joint computation using that fixed producer:

* With no extra b'-zero supplier gate, the fixed-supplier certificate applies directly.
* With one extra supplier gate, only ten b'-bearing gates remain. Their entries must all be clean. An exit cannot supply the first clean direction outside W by the causal lemma. A gate reading two original zero-b' values cannot do so either: a clean sum of two nonclean values is a within-coset difference and lies in W; a sum of clean values also lies in W.

The producer already costs ten and the consumer needs at least ten, so there is no third split within a total of twenty-one. The explicit twenty-two-gate extension in EXPORTS attains the lower bound for this fixed producer. The statement allows consumer-block exits; it does not impose the stronger condition that every extra supplier must have source-only ancestry.

The earlier one-diagonal producer in [JOINT01](../row-cost/JOINT01.md) also satisfies the fixed-supplier deficiency certificate: its largest nonzero coset has three values whose difference span is two-dimensional and contains its one clean direction; every other coset adds at most one direction. With one extra supplier, the clean span can rise from dimension one to at most two, which is still insufficient for a tight ten-gate consumer. Its previously proved exclusion remains valid.
