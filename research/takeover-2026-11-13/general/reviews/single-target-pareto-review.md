## The same representation controls both resources

The supplied dictionary is a set of actual nonzero values; its dependence is
permitted. Giving each member a fresh formal coordinate lifts the fixed circuit
without changing its parent pointers or any gate parity. Projection of its final
formal value gives the physical target, so the formal support selects a subset
R of the actual dictionary representing that target. Since the target is
nonzero, this subset is nonempty. Crucially it is selected before either price
is chosen.

The assigned odd weight (alpha+beta)/2 and even weight beta give every physically
available nonzero value exactly its parity weight. An odd representation needs
an odd term. A nonzero even representation needs an even term or at least two
odd terms, whose combined weight is alpha+beta>=beta. This includes zero prices.
The exact dictionary update follows by deciding whether to use the newly
available value. Substituting its parents shows that an odd-plus-odd operation
can lower the weighted length by at most alpha and either other operation by
at most beta.

In the lifted dictionary, input independence makes the initial weighted length
of the final value exactly its selected support weight. At completion its length
is its parity weight. The resulting inequality is

    alpha*K+beta*P >= alpha*(o-I)/2
                        + beta*((o+I)/2+e-1).

Using the two coordinate price choices proves K_R<=K and P_R<=P for the same R.
This is stronger than separate optimization or a convex-hull statement. A
physical zero gate may lift to a nonzero formal even value, but this does not
change its parity or its charged gate type. A formal zero result is inert and
cannot violate the price bound.

## Attainment and exact discrete scope

Pairing odd dictionary leaves and then combining the resulting even operands
with the remaining leaves gives exactly the displayed K_R,P_R. For an odd target
one odd leaf is retained as an anchor; for an even target all odd leaves are
paired. The formulas use each dictionary member once. Actual dependence can
create a zero intermediate but cannot alter the parity counts, and zero values
are explicitly allowed in the model. Both coordinates are nonnegative for
any nonempty representation of a nonzero target.

Every representation point is attainable, and every circuit is dominated by
one such point. Thus the nondominated points are exactly the stated discrete
frontier. For equal unit prices this reduces correctly to the minimum actual
dictionary representation size minus one. For the one-sided price it recovers
the earlier combined-resource theorem. No claim that the representation problem
is computationally easy is made.
