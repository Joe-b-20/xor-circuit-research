# Review of the one-mixed-auxiliary restriction

2026-11-13. Independent machine proof review of [AUXILIARY](../AUXILIARY.md). No circuit search or family checks were run.

The four required rows and three independent diagonals are independent as seven full values. Taking the difference of the own and free components kills each diagonal and maps the row coefficient vector \(u\) to \((I+J)Pu\), which is invertible. Hence a representation of the mixed auxiliary \(H\) as the sum of two distinct required values determines the unordered pair uniquely. At most one required gate can use \(H\) and the other member of that pair, because only the later member can be a gate result of that equation. If both members were used to make \(H\), neither can be a later result.

The three nonzero even masks outside the four cycle pairs are \(02,13,0123\). They form the nonzero part of a plane. Two different noncycle diagonal masks therefore sum to another noncycle mask, and an independent diagonal basis cannot contain all three. Each chosen noncycle diagonal has its own and free parts distinct from those of every other required value. Event-freeness preserves both blocks along every downstream path. The first reader on a path to a row must therefore be mixed. A required reader cannot use the noncycle diagonal and a pure or free operand, since one of its two parts would remain unchanged. It cannot use two required operands by independence. With one auxiliary, the first reader must be \(H\), or a required gate using \(H\) as its other operand.

If \(H\) has no required operand, both noncycle diagonals would require separate services, exceeding its one-service capacity. If it has two required operands, it has no service at all, so the two noncycle diagonals must be its operands. Their sum is a noncycle diagonal different from any required diagonal by independence. No required reader can preserve either part of this sum, and no service is possible. This would leave the required diagonals with no path through \(H\) to a row.

If \(H\) has one required operand, that operand must be one of the two selected noncycle diagonals. Write \(H=X+p\), where \(p\) is pure-own or free. The other diagonal \(Y\) needs a service, giving a required result \(Q=H+Y\). The result is distinct from \(X,Y\) by order and reducedness. Therefore \(p=X+Y+Q\) is mixed. For a row \(Q\), its own part is odd and its free part is the sum of a cycle pair with a noncycle mask, so neither vanishes. For a diagonal \(Q\), it is the third independent required diagonal, so the sum is a nonzero diagonal. Both contradict purity of \(p\). This completes the one-auxiliary exclusion.

The no-mixed-auxiliary theorem in [AUGMENTED](../../frontier/AUGMENTED.md) supplies the zero-auxiliary exclusion at eleven gates. Under the present assumption that the two noncycle diagonals feed rows, zero auxiliaries also fail directly by the same first-reader argument. The ancestry hypothesis for hypothetical eleven-gate augmented circuits follows from [ODD_CLOSURE](../ODD_CLOSURE.md). The partial six-gate example checks by XOR expansion and shows why a single noncycle diagonal can have such a first reader. The result is restricted to event-free circuits with the specified diagonal profile and gives no general exclusion of eleven gates.
