# Review of the rank-two normal form

2026-11-13. Independent machine proof review of [NORMAL_FORM](../NORMAL_FORM.md). No circuit search or family checks were run.

Let \(F\) annihilate \(W_b\) on the even own space, with coordinate vector \(w\), and let \(t\) be the full parity. The two classes of named odd values each have four nodes. If \(F(t)=0\), the nonconstant \(w\) has two ones and two zeros, and input and output class counts match. If \(F(t)=1\), output classes are the opposite of the corresponding input classes. The sum of all named residues is zero, so the two class sums agree. Computing the class-one sum gives \(w\) from inputs. For outputs, the coefficient of residue \(t+Pe_c\) is \(F(t)+w_c\); the total \(t\) contribution is \(F(t)t\), and the shift contribution is \(F(t)t+Pw\). Thus the class sum is \(w+Pw\), nonzero because a cyclically fixed \(w\) is constant.

Let \(V\) be the span of even physical values and \(R=\rho(V)\). Theorem E2 confines its own masks to \(W_b\), so the kernel of \(\rho\) on \(V\) is exactly the two-dimensional \(W\). The relation-rank bound gives \(c+\dim R\le\tau+2\), where \(c\) counts odd graph components. Its edges preserve own class and residue modulo \(R\). Rank zero of \(R\) would separate all eight named residues. Rank one gives four residue pairs, at least two of which split across own classes: each own class can contain at most one whole pair because its four-point sum is nonzero. Thus there are at least six components, again too many. At rank two, the two four-point residue classes each meet both own classes, since a full affine plane has zero sum. This gives \(c\ge4\), forcing \(\tau=4\) and \(c=4\). The even span needs at least four physical gates, hence at most two odd helpers.

With one helper, the five even values are independent if \(\dim R=3\). If \(\dim R=2\), there are four entries, and the six first occurrences are the four distinct own triples and two independent even own masks. In that case an even-only equation is impossible because three nonzero masks drawn from two independent masks cannot sum to zero. Exactly two even values are clean in either case. The clean pair count is at most one, because the single helper has a unique same-residue named point. A nonclean value has at most one named pair and one helper pair. When both pairs exist, they express the helper as a sum of the same unique triple of named values. The two formal equations for each such nonclean value sum to the same formal vector, supported on the helper and the triple. Thus the equations for \(j\) doubled values span at most \(j+1\) dimensions. Including all other equations leaves rank at most five, below the six required. This excludes one helper.

The earlier no-helper proof excludes zero, so the two-helper conclusion follows. The four physical even values then have rank four, the residue rank is two, and there are four entries and six first occurrences. There are two odd entries and two even entries by counting each parity. This does not identify the odd-entry nodes with the two odd non-output helpers: a designated output can be an entry after an earlier first occurrence of its own triple.


## Distribution of entries by class

The six physical non-entry equations form a forest on ten odd nodes: any cycle would use each independent physical even colour evenly and give a formal relation dependency. Its four components coincide with the four occupied combinations of own class and residue coset. After projection, the two even own masks are independent, so the six projected equations also give a forest, now on eight odd own masks. Its two components are the two own classes. Adding the odd-entry edges before projection connects the own fibres, and contracting those fibres gives the two projected trees. Thus the odd-entry edges must link the two components in each class. There are two edges, giving exactly one per class. Each class has one extra odd physical node by the parity-specific first-occurrence count. A helper may be the first node of an output fibre, while the output is the entry.

The two even-entry labels are differences of physical even values, so their masks lie in \(R\) under the residue map. The even values span their two roots and the two even-entry labels, and the roots have independent own parts. Rank four therefore makes the labels independent. They are a basis of \(R\). Each odd-entry edge connects distinct residue cosets modulo \(R\), so its label is outside \(R\).
