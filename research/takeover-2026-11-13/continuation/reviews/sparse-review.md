# Review of the sparse-boundary hub family

2026-11-13. Independent machine proof review of the saved packed-block construction in [SPARSE](../SPARSE.md). No circuit search, enumeration, or finite-family checks were run.

## Construction and boundary relations

The two common pairs on \(X,Y\) and their sum use three gates. In group \(i\), the three \(Y\)-bearing prefixes cost three gates, the three private attachments cost \(4+3+2=9\), and the gate \(T_i\) costs one. Hence the specified circuit has \(13r+3\) gates. Each prefix reaches a designated \(A_{ik}\) without a later event, as does the pure \(Y\) pair. Only \(T_i\) gates cancel a whole block. The pure \(X\) pair and the sum of the two common pairs reach outputs only through a \(T_i\) event. Thus \(H=2\), both hidden gates are non-events, and only the latter gate is nonlocal. Distinct supports or distinct within-block prefixes distinguish all computed masks. Every gate is used.

Each private block \(W_{ik}\) is used by its own \(A_{ik}\) output, whose window is \(\{Y,Z_i,W_{ik}\}\). Each \(T_i\) has window \(\{X,Z_i\}\). There is no edge \(X-Y\) or \(X-W_{ik}\), and each \(W_{ik}\) is adjacent only to \(Y,Z_i\). These windows are maximal cliques, and the own blocks \(W_{ik},Z_i\) yield no reciprocal incidences. Window size is at most three and block dimension at most four. Packing the private coordinates changes the block geometry without changing any coordinate-unit relation. The family is rectangular. All rows are independent: each \(A_{ik}\) has a coordinate unique to it, and after their coefficients vanish, the separate \(Z_i\) blocks separate the \(T_i\).

For any nonempty subset of outputs, a boundary relation using that subset must include precisely the input units in its XOR mask. With only \(k\) outputs \(T_i\), the mask has weight \(3k\) plus two when \(k\) is odd, so the relation length is at least six. With at least two outputs of type \(A\), each contributes at least two unique private units, so the number of chosen outputs and private units alone is at least six. With exactly one \(A\) and at least one \(T\), the mask contains the two \(Y\) units and at least two private units, in addition to the at least two output terms. A lone \(A\) has mask weight seven. A relation using only coordinate inputs cannot be nonzero. The minimum length is therefore exactly six, attained by a weight-five \(T_i\) and its five input units.

## Removing coordinates in packed private blocks

At each restriction step, work with the current circuit obtained by deleting gates and redirecting equal projected values. The affected \(A_{ik}\) still depends on the next private coordinate \(p\), and retains the two \(Y\) coordinates and at least one \(Z_i\) coordinate. It is therefore neither an input wire nor zero. A path from \(p\) to that output starts with a gate reading \(p\) directly. The earliest live direct reader also has the dependency property stated in SPARSE: a path from \(p\) through its other operand would contain an earlier live direct reader. Once \(p=0\), this gate can be bypassed to the projected value of its other operand. Even if the other operand also becomes zero, the gate can be discarded and its zero uses simplified. All designated outputs stay nonzero, so zero values need no retained generating gate. There is at least one fewer physical gate at each step.

Individual coordinate restrictions can create whole-block events inside a packed block. The counting argument for one-coordinate deletion does not require event-freeness of the intermediate circuits. Maintain the invariant that each surviving gate is an original physical gate and that its current operand masks are the current projections of its original operand masks. Projecting further preserves equality of redirected wires, and all simplifications either delete a gate or redirect an operand to an equal projected value. This proves the invariant inductively. After all private blocks are removed, the projection is a whole-block restriction. Any final gate whose operands agreed nontrivially on a surviving block would correspond to an original gate with the same nonzero agreement there. Since the original circuit is event-free, the final circuit is event-free. This justifies removal of at least \(9r\) gates without assuming that the first readers of different private coordinates in the original circuit were distinct.

## Reduced event-free problem

The \(3r\) distinct projected outputs \(P_{ik}\) contain the \(Y\) pair and require distinct gates. The first \(Y\)-bearing gate in the reduced circuit is an additional gate. If it reads a single \(Y\) input, it has one \(Y\) coordinate, so its value is not any \(P_{ik}\). If it reads two \(Y\) inputs, it has no \(Z_i\) content, so again it is not a \(P_{ik}\). Its output cannot lie in a \(T_i\) cone, because support growth excludes \(Y\) from those cones.

A \(T_i\) has five independent coordinates in its support, so its ancestor cone has at least four binary gates. Distinct \(T_i\) cones can share only gates supported on the two-coordinate block \(X\). In a reduced circuit the pure \(X\) pair is the only possible such gate. If it occurs in the union, count it once and at least three further distinct gates per cone. If it does not occur, the cones are disjoint and supply \(4r\ge3r+1\) gates. Thus the union contributes at least \(3r+1\) gates, disjoint from the \(3r+1\) gates just charged on \(Y\). This proves the reduced lower bound \(6r+2\). The two pure pairs, two clean \(Z_i\) prefix gates per group, the three \(P_{ik}\) gates per group, and one \(T_i\) per group attain it. Restoring the private attachments gives exactly \(15r+2\) event-free gates.

The resulting advantage over the displayed \(13r+3\) circuit is at least \(2r-1\). This compares the explicit circuit with the exact event-free optimum and requires no claim about the unrestricted optimum.

## Affine control

The selected \(T_i\) gates share the one nonlocal operand \(a\). Each value of \(a+\operatorname{span}\{T_i\}\) contains \(Y_{\rm sum}\). To be local it must omit \(X\) and can meet at most one \(Z_i\). This forces exactly one \(T_i\) in the affine sum, giving the completion \(Y_{\rm sum}+S_i\). The \(r-1\) differences from the first completion are independent on their separate \(Z_i\) blocks. Thus AC gives one nonlocal operand and is attained. The other hidden gate is the local \(X_{\rm sum}\), so it is outside the quotient count.
