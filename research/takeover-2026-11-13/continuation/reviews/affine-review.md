# Independent review of affine-completion accounting

Hand review of `continuation/AFFINE.md`; no search, enumeration, exact gate search, or computer-driven case elimination was used. This is not formal verification.

Theorem AC, the stronger no-local-partner case AC0, the parity-incidence refinement AC+, and the affine-intersection formula are correct under their stated hypotheses. In particular the physical nonlocal-operand components are disjoint, so their bounds can be summed without charging a shared operand twice.

For a component, every vertex belongs to `a+K`: along an edge, its two endpoint values differ by a selected target value. The local partner of an assigned one-nonlocal-operand gate also belongs to that coset. If the geometrically eligible set is nonempty, choose a local anchor `ell` and translate each vertex to `z_v=val(v)+ell` in `K`. Modulo the span of local-completion differences, each target is either one such translated vertex or the sum of two, according to whether the other operand is local or nonlocal. Consequently the translated vertices span `K/Delta`, giving `h>=dim(K)-dim(Delta)`.

If there are no actual local-partner gates, all target values are sums of pairs of vertex values. Their coefficient vectors lie in the even-weight subspace of `F_2^h`, of dimension `h-1`, so `h>=dim(K)+1`. The same conclusion follows if there are no geometrically possible local completions. Lifting each translated vertex to `([z_v],1)` gives the AC+ parity bound; its rank can exceed the quotient dimension by at most one.

The stated nonenumerative formula for `Delta` follows by writing each nonempty coset/window intersection as `ell_W+(K intersect V_W)`. The sum must range over windows with nonempty intersections. In the symbolic multiwindow example, these are the windows containing `Y`, and each such eligible intersection has zero direction space; their representatives differ by the independent vectors `S_i+S_1`. In the initial version, the phrase “each within-window intersection `K intersect V_W` is zero” needed the qualifier “eligible”: a selected window `{X,Z_i}` contains `T_i`, although it does not meet the affine completion coset because that coset contains `y`. The author was notified. The computed quotient rank one remains correct.

For the explicit MixColumns overlap example of `OVERLAP.md`, the actual shared hidden operand has three geometrically local completions `b=a+F_0`, `c=a+G_0`, and `s_7(0)=a+F_0+G_0`. Their pairwise differences span all of `K=span{F_0,G_0}`, so AC gives zero there even though one hidden gate exists. This is a limitation, not a counterexample to AC; the usual single-window lemma supplies the missing gate separately. It also shows that local values which are geometrically eligible but not actual partners can materially weaken the quotient.

The relation to the prior HR theorem is valid: when all selected targets lie in one maximal window, every completion in the same coset retains the component's fixed nonzero projection outside that window. The separated coordinates incompatible with that projection vanish on every geometrically local completion, so their projection kills `Delta` and factors through `K/Delta`.
