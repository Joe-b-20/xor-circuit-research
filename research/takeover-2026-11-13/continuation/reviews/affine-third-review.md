# Independent review of the affine-completion theorem

2026-11-13. Machine review by the structural-direction agent. This is a hand proof audit, not human review or formal verification. No search, value enumeration, circuit optimization, or computer-driven case elimination was used.

## Verdict

Theorem AC, Theorem AC0, the AC+ incidence-bit refinement, the linear-algebra formula for `Delta`, and the comparison with HR are correct as stated. Two explanatory corrections are required: only **eligible-window** intersections `K intersect V_W` vanish in the hub example; and the translated quotient is equivalent in dimension to the ambient quotient by `span(E)`, rather than stronger. The second correction, caught by the primary agent after this review, is proved in the appended note below. Neither correction changes AC or its proof.

## Proof checks

1. **Component and coset.** Every nonlocal vertex of one operand component differs from a reference vertex by a sum of selected edge values, so it lies in `a_i+K_i`. A local partner differs from its incident vertex by a selected value, so it lies in the same coset. Assigning an event to the component is unambiguous even with two nonlocal operands, since that event joins them.

2. **Translation to `K_i`.** If `ell_i` is a geometrically possible local completion, every shifted vertex `val(v)+ell_i` lies in `K_i`. For an actual local partner `b`, its shift `b+ell_i` is one of the generators of `Delta_i`. The selected event equations therefore project to either one or two shifted vertex images. Their images span `K_i/Delta_i`, giving the claimed dimension bound. No linear span of all free inputs is introduced.

3. **No-local-partner improvement.** When every assigned selected gate has two nonlocal operands, all its formal coefficient vectors lie in the even subspace of the `h_i` vertex-coordinate space. This has dimension `h_i-1`, so `r_i<=h_i-1` without any independence or acyclicity assumption on selected values. If `E_i` is empty, the absence of actual local partners follows immediately. The stated separate version when their absence is known directly is also valid.

4. **Incidence bit.** A one-vertex equation is represented by one vector `([z_v],1)`, whereas a two-vertex equation is represented by two such vectors and hence has final coordinate zero. The lifted selected rows are therefore in the span of the `h_i` lifted vertex vectors. Appending a single coordinate increases the selected-row rank by at most one. The bit need not be a linear functional of the unlifted selected values; taking the rank of the displayed lifted rows correctly handles that possibility.

5. **Finite description.** An eligible affine intersection has exactly the form `ell_W+(K_i intersect V_W)`. Differences from a fixed eligible anchor are generated by the within-window subspaces and the representative differences. Conversely each within-window direction is a difference of two actual completions in that window, so the proposed span is exactly `Delta_i`, rather than merely an upper bound. Choice of representatives and anchor changes no resulting span.

6. **Relation to HR.** A projection annihilating all possible local completions annihilates all their differences. Its restriction to `K_i` consequently factors through the quotient, giving the rank comparison. In the earlier common-window HR setting, the fixed nonzero outside-window component ensures the same separated-block projection annihilates all possible local completions, not merely those actually used. Thus the stated inclusion of HR is justified.

7. **Summation.** Operand components partition the physical nonlocal vertices, so their lower bounds can be added without summing separate copies of one gate. Every nonlocal gate is hidden because an event-free continuation would keep its support inside a designated output window. This last conclusion uses the existing block-support growth argument and does not require the selected gates themselves to be designated outputs.

## Hub-family check and requested correction

In `MULTIWINDOW.md`, let `S_i` be the clean sum on `Z_i`. Then

    K=span{x+S_i},       a=x+y.

Every member of `a+K` has a nonzero `Y` component. Locality therefore excludes the target windows `{X,Z_i}`. In an eligible auxiliary window `{Y,Z_i,W_ik}`, the `X` component must vanish and at most one `Z` block can remain; hence the only local completion in that window is `y+S_i`. The completion set is exactly the listed `r` values, whose difference span has dimension `r-1`. AC gives exactly one hidden gate, attained by the construction.

The claim that *every* intersection `K intersect V_W` is zero would be false: `x+S_i` belongs to `K intersect V_{ {X,Z_i} }`. These target windows are ineligible because no element of the affine coset can lose its `Y` component. The intended statement, and the one needed for the switch-only conclusion, is that every **eligible-window** intersection is zero. This correction has been sent to the author.

## Scope controls

* If `K` is the entire coordinate space, then the affine coset is also the entire space. Its local completion set includes zero and every coordinate input. Consequently `Delta=K` and AC gives zero. AC+ gives at most one unless the separate no-actual-local-partner argument applies. This is a useful limitation, not a defect in the proof.
* The actual MixColumns overlap example in `OVERLAP.md` has additional geometrically possible completions. Their differences can span its entire selected-value space, so the basic quotient may be zero even though the circuit has a hidden gate. Exactness on the hub family must not be presented as exactness on all circuits.
* The theorem makes an explicit, sound lower bound from circuit-constrained operand equations. It does not prove that every quotient direction requires an additional gate outside a separate event-free bound, nor does it establish a new unrestricted MixColumns lower bound.

## Appended correction: translation and the ambient quotient have equal dimension

The primary agent correctly identified an explanatory error missed in my initial review. The original paragraph after the proof of AC claimed that quotienting the ambient space by `span(E)` could unnecessarily discard the local anchor when `a` belongs to `K`, and that translation gave a stronger bound in that case. That claim is false. In every nonempty-completion case,

    K intersect span(E) = Delta.

Here is the complete verification.

* If `a in K`, then `a+K=K`. Zero is local, so `0 in E`. Choosing zero as the local anchor gives `Delta=span(E)`, which is contained in `K`.
* If `a notin K`, fix `ell_0 in E`. Then `ell_0 notin K`, and every completion has the form `ell_0+d` with `d in Delta`. Conversely all the generators of `Delta` are sums of two completions. Therefore `span(E)=Delta+span{ell_0}`, and this sum is direct because `Delta` is contained in `K` while `ell_0` is not. Its intersection with `K` is exactly `Delta`.

Equivalently, in the second case an odd sum of completions remains in `a+K` and cannot lie in `K`; an even sum is a sum of completion differences. Consequently the image of `K` in the ambient quotient by `span(E)` has dimension

    dim K - dim(K intersect span(E)) = dim K - dim Delta,

exactly the dimension used by AC. Translation is a convenient presentation inside `K` and supports the incidence-bit formulation; no strict improvement over that ambient quotient is established or needed. The proof of AC and all numerical consequences remain unchanged.
