# Second independent review: packed sparse boundary-distance counterexample

Hand review of the final packed-block version of `../SPARSE.md`: the three private blocks per group have dimensions four, three, and two; the largest output window has three blocks. No SAT, exhaustive enumeration, exact-gate search, or computer-driven case elimination was used. The exact event-free complexity follows from a symbolic lower bound and matching construction, not search. This review supersedes my earlier review of the temporary singleton-block version.

## Verdict

The explicit `13r+3`-gate construction, its two hidden gates, all stated output and window properties, the exact event-free minimum `15r+2`, and the boundary relation distance six are correct for every `r>=1`. The map has `12r+4` input coordinates and `4r` independent outputs, so is rectangular; the note correctly does not claim an invertible or actual MixColumns counterexample. For fixed block dimension and window width, the construction gives an event-free separation of at least `2r-1` with constant hidden count two.

The final proof correctly permits intermediate coordinate restrictions to create events. Its gate-loss and normalization invariant do not require those intermediate circuits to be event-free. Event-freeness is recovered only after all private coordinates have been zeroed, when the cumulative restriction deletes complete private blocks.

## Construction and geometry

`Xsum`, `Ysum`, and `a=Xsum+Ysum` cost three gates. The three retained prefixes, nine private attachments, and one target event in each group cost `13r` more. Each prefix and attachment has an event-free continuation to an `A_ik` output; `Ysum` does too. The only paths from `Xsum` and `a` to outputs go through a `T_i` event, so these are exactly the two hidden gates, both non-events. The gate `a` is the only nonlocal gate. Every other intermediate is contained in a suitable output window. Every gate value is distinct and non-input and every gate is live.

`A_ik` has weight `2+k+(5-k)=7` and `T_i` has weight five. Independent private coordinates force all coefficients of `A` outputs to vanish in a dependency, and disjoint `Z_i` supports then force all coefficients of `T` outputs to vanish. Each `V_i={X,Z_i}` is a maximal clique, since `X` is adjacent only to the `Z_j`, which are pairwise nonadjacent. Own blocks `Z_i` and `W_ik` are distinct, and the stated absence of reciprocal own-block incidence follows directly. Each `A` window is `{Y,Z_i,W_ik}`; the maximum window width is three and the maximum block dimension is four.

## Sequential restrictions and lower bound

At each step, the private coordinate being removed still appears in its designated `A_ik` output, which also retains the nonzero expression `Ysum+S_ik` and hence is not that coordinate input. In the current live, normalized XOR circuit the coordinate must be read by a gate on a path to that output. Take its earliest live direct reader. Its other operand cannot depend on this coordinate through an earlier gate, since such a gate would entail an earlier direct reader. If its other operand were the same input, the gate would have zero value and would already have been removed by normalization. Setting the coordinate to zero therefore makes this gate a wire or zero, so at least one current physical gate disappears. This argument uses no event-free property of the intermediate circuit.

After every restriction, bypassing zero/copy gates, redirecting a node to an earlier equal current value, and pruning preserve the current projected value of every surviving node and of each of its original operands. An equality after one projection remains an equality after further projection. Thus, after all restrictions, every surviving physical gate has operands with the original operand values projected onto the surviving whole blocks. An event in this final circuit would require equality of two nonzero operand components on a surviving block, which would have been the same equality for the original event-free gate. This proves the final circuit is event-free. In particular, the proof does not claim that a partially zeroed private block preserves event-freeness.

At least `9r` gates are lost and the final circuit computes all `P_ik=Ysum+S_ik` and `T_i`. The `3r` distinct `P_ik` outputs require `3r` distinct `Y`-bearing gates. An additional earlier `Y`-bearing gate is necessary: if the earliest `P` gate had no earlier `Y`-bearing gate operand, the only available operands with nonzero `Y` components would be the individual inputs `y_1,y_2`; combining those two yields no `Z_i` component. Thus at least `3r+1` `Y`-bearing gates exist.

Each `T_i` has a final event-free gate cone supported inside `{X,Z_i}`, so it is disjoint from those `Y`-bearing gates. Its five essential input coordinates require at least four binary gates in the cone. Distinct target cones intersect only in gates supported on `X`. After reduction, the only possible such gate is the unique non-input nonzero `X` value `Xsum`. Therefore their union has at least `4r-(r-1)=3r+1` gates. Together these counts give `6r+2`; adding the `9r` removed gates yields `15r+2` for the original event-free circuit. The note's clean-prefix construction attains exactly this number.

## Boundary distance and affine control

For a nonempty set of selected output vectors, the only input subset that can complete a relation is the support of their XOR. If at least two `A` outputs are selected, each contributes at least two private coordinates, already forcing relation weight at least six. If exactly one `A` and at least one `T` are selected, its private coordinates and both `Y` coordinates survive, again giving at least six. A lone `A` gives eight; a nonempty set of `t` targets `T_i` gives relation weight `4t+2[t odd]>=6` because the `Z_i` triples are disjoint. A single `T_i` plus its five input coordinates attains six. This argument applies symbolically to arbitrary subsets; it is not a subset enumeration.

For affine completion, every vector in `a+span(T_i)` contains `Ysum`. A local vector cannot also contain `X`, and a window containing `Y` contains at most one `Z_i`. Thus the local completions are exactly `Ysum+S_i`; their differences have rank `r-1`. The affine quotient yields one nonlocal gate, as attained by `a`. The second hidden gate, `Xsum`, is local and correctly lies outside that nonlocal count.
