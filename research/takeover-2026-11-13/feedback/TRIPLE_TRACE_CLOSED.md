# A trace cannot occur three times in a cheaper six-entry consumer

2026-11-14. Structural hand proof by trace-relation rank, marked helper wires, physical difference pairs, and chronological source replay. No circuit search, trace enumeration, or computer-driven elimination.

**Theorem.** In a joint circuit of total at most 21 with twelve consumer-bearing gates, no entry trace occurs three or more times.

Use the [current six-entry reduction](SIX_ENTRY_FRONTIER.md): all twelve gates lie in the relaxed target cones, there are six entries, their actual labels span a rank-five space `U`, and the source count is `G_0<=9`. The trace matrix `Z` has rank four. Its two-dimensional kernel is the quotient-signature row space, and the unique actual-label relation is the indicator of odd trace positions. There are at most two original nonzero physical clean values. Saturated quotient rigidity forbids zero labels, duplicate physical consumer values, and repeated nonclean labels, and makes each actual nonclean label's entry edge its unique physical difference pair.

## A triple trace forces a repeated-clean profile

A trace class of size `s` supplies `s-1` independent relations supported on that class. Thus `s<=3`. Suppose a class has size three. Its two pair relations exhaust `ker Z`. After choosing quotient coordinates, the three actual labels in that class have the three different nonzero quotient signatures, while every other actual label is clean. The remaining three traces, together with the repeated trace, are independent and distinct.

If the repeated trace were odd, the unique actual-label relation would include all three nonclean occurrences. The three clean occurrences could then have no relation among themselves. In particular they would be three distinct nonzero physical clean values, contradicting the cap of two.

The repeated trace is therefore even. The actual relation lies among the other three clean occurrences. Their rank is two, because there is exactly one relation among all six occurrences, and the cap of two distinct physical clean values forces their values to be

    c,c,d,

with `c,d` independent. Their unique relation is the pair of `c` occurrences. Consequently the two `c` traces are odd, and the `d` trace is the other even helper, different from the repeated trace.

Write the three nonclean labels as `a,b,k`. The five distinct values `a,b,k,c,d` are independent. Because the four distinct trace values are independent, the target source space is generated by their actual class sums, so

    S=a+b+k,       D=span{c,d,S}.

In particular `S` is independent of `c,d`. The original physical clean set contains only `c,d`: both already occur, and the cap allows no third nonzero clean value. Thus no source exit can return `S` or `c+S`.

## Equal even traces in one fibre form a chain

The ordinary projection and marked mirror both have six gates. Each original ordinary-mask fibre is one entry tree. In the mirror, a fixed even helper is read only by odd target gates. Their full ordinary adjoints are independent, so a nonzero ordinary derivative in that helper's copy tree identifies its downstream ordinary-reader subset uniquely.

All marked positions for one fixed nonempty subset lie on one path segment: nodes with overlapping downstream leaves are nested, and between two nodes with the same subset there is no ordinary-reader split. Any trace ports on that segment carry the same even helper value. Transposing back therefore makes all entries of that even trace and ordinary mask one connected entry chain.

This fact does not require the helper to have at most two readers. A segment between two internal merges still gives a chain, even when neither endpoint is known clean.

Apply this to the common trace of `a,b,k`. Their entries in any one fibre form a chain. The marked `d` trace is the different even helper; even-wire separation puts it in another fibre. Thus a fibre containing nonclean entries consists of a chain of at most three of `a,b,k`, with any of its `c` entries attached to that chain.

The two `c` edges cannot meet: their outer endpoints would have equal full values. They cannot attach at adjacent vertices of the nonclean chain either. The path `c-ell-c` would give a second physical difference pair of the actual nonclean label `ell`. Therefore, if both `c` edges lie in one nonclean fibre, they are leaf edges attached at chain vertices separated by at least two nonclean edges.

Fibres with no nonclean entry contain only occurrences from `c,c,d`. Every exit there is zero, an original label, or a sum of at most two original labels: the only possible three-edge path is `c-d-c`, whose sum is `d`.

## All difficult returns share one source helper

Every exit is the sum of labels on its fibre path, all read before the exit. In a nonclean fibre, a path uses an interval of its nonclean chain and zero, one, or two `c` edges.

- With zero or two `c` edges, its value is a sum of nonclean labels. An interval of at most two costs at most one XOR; an interval of all three would return the forbidden clean value `S`.
- With one `c` edge, zero or one nonclean label again costs at most one XOR. All three would return the forbidden clean value `c+S`.

Only a value `c` plus two consecutive nonclean labels can need an extra source gate. Call such a return difficult; this names its path expression, without assuming it has no alternative cheap realization.

There are at most two distinct difficult values, and if there are two, they share one nonclean summand. To see this structurally, a `c` attachment has at most one length-two direction along a chain of at most three edges. Two different fibres cannot each contain a length-two nonclean interval, because there are only three nonclean entries in total. A chain of length two gives just one pair sum. Hence two distinct difficult values require all three nonclean entries in one chain. Its two length-two intervals overlap in the middle edge. The separation of the two `c` attachments forces them to the chain's two outer ends. The full fibre is consequently

    c - a - b - k - c,

after naming the middle nonclean label `b`, and the two difficult values are

    f=c+a+b,       g=c+b+k.

Retain the single helper `t=c+b`. Then `f=t+a` and `g=t+k` each use at most their original exit XOR. Both `c` and `b` were read before either difficult return that needs this helper. If there is only one difficult value, choose either of its nonclean terms as `b` and use the same one-helper repair.

Replay ordinary source gates chronologically, replacing every exit by the path expression just described and retaining repeated values. All nondifficult exits cost at most their original one gate, and all difficult exits together require at most one extra helper. Every original source value is therefore preserved in a source-only circuit of at most `G_0+1` gates. Other source gates may have had feedback ancestors; the path-label availability gives the required induction.

## The diagonal supplier contradiction

If no difficult return occurs, the replay needs no extra gate. It would compute the four source rows within `G_0<=9` gates, contradicting their local minimum ten.

Otherwise retain one difficult return, say `f=c+a+b`, and let `k` be the unused nonclean label. After replay all actual labels are available. One additional XOR supplies

    f+k=c+S.

The source-only circuit now supplies the four source rows and the independent clean values `c,d,c+S` within

    G_0+2<=11

gates. The [three-diagonal supplier bound A12](../augmented/PROOF.md) requires at least twelve gates. This contradiction excludes the triple trace, completing the theorem.

The argument leaves trace classes of size two open. It also gives a general marked-wire fact: repeated uses of one even trace in a fixed ordinary-mask fibre form a chain, even when the helper has three ordinary readers and clean endpoints are unavailable.
