The interface theorem gives five distinct traces and a single label relation supported on odd traces. Exactly two of the three nonclean traces are odd, while at least one clean trace is odd: otherwise the two distinct nonclean labels in that relation would be equal. The two nonzero clean labels `c,d` are distinct, since equality would furnish a different label relation, and hence are independent. Their relation with the two odd-trace nonclean labels takes the form `n1+n2=t`, with `t` one of `c,d,c+d`. The rank-four label span forces `e=n1+k` outside `span{c,d}`.

For chronological replay, naming `n1` as the earlier read of the related pair ensures that both physical source values are present when `n2` is read. If the clean value `t` has not yet been supplied, the replay can compute it as `n1+n2` immediately, so that `n2` has an available decomposition `n1+t`. Closing the available part of the clean plane is necessary: for example, if `t=c+d` appears before either basis value, then later availability of `c` also makes `d` computable before its original occurrence. The note correctly performs that completion rather than assuming the future basis value is already free. In the other group the only possible nontrivial completion is `e=n1+k`. Since every actual entry is then decomposed into physically available group components, linear closure and the causal exit invariant give the same property for each source exit. Its final combination uses at most its original gate allowance.

The cost accounting is valid. A synthetically advanced `c` or `d` is an original nonzero diagonal source value, so it has a paid zero-consumer producer; if that producer had already been replayed its value would already be available. Thus a genuinely early computation of either has its own later gate bypass, and the two credits are distinct. The clean pair `c+d` and the other group sum `e` each contribute at most one uncredited gate, even if completed after the original replay. All original source values are retained. The resulting supplier has at most `G_0+2` gates and supplies the four rows with the triangle `c,d,c+d` and the independent fourth diagonal `e`, so the thirteen-gate supplier bound gives `G_0>=11`.

The five-entry trace theorem is applied to the relaxed consumer target cones, and the conclusion explicitly leaves four- and five-nonclean entry classes and other consumer budgets open. The more general replay observation depends on the same stated label geometry and physical occurrence assumptions; it does not assume an arbitrary rank-four interface has that geometry.
