# Independent review: four- and five-nonclean frontier

I reviewed [FOUR_FIVE_FRONTIER.md](../FOUR_FIVE_FRONTIER.md), including the all-five-odd trace possibility, the pre-outside-label replay, and the physical clean-rank conclusion for the three-term relation. I found no material gap. This review used the stated symbolic relations and did not search circuits.

The quotient trace theorem gives one label relation with odd-trace support, while its separate trace relation is supported on the nonclean labels. For four nonclean entries, the number with odd traces is two or four. With two, the other two consume the available even traces, forcing the sole clean trace to be odd and giving the three-term relation. With four, the clean trace may be even or odd at the level of the stated algebraic constraints. These give respectively the four-nonclean plane relation and the five-term relation. In the latter, the trace relation involves the four nonclean traces only; it does not force the fifth odd trace into that trace relation. The four nonclean labels form a basis because the sole label relation includes the clean label. The note properly presents this as an algebraic possibility without asserting a consumer realization. The all-nonclean case correctly has a four-label affine plane plus the fifth point outside its linear span.

Before the first read of the outside-plane label, causal entry-span containment confines every exit to the span of earlier plane labels. At most two such labels have a span whose members are present or one XOR away. Three distinct plane labels have pairwise differences equal to the three nonzero directions of the plane, so every clean value in that span is one XOR away. Its nonclean values are precisely the four actual plane entry labels. The eleven-gate collision result rules out a nonredundant exit returning any such label, including one read only later. This yields the claimed cost-preserving replay without requiring the outside label's physical source producer to occur late.

For the three-term relation, the first group consists of the three original values `n1,n2,c`; supplying a missing member early from the other two is credited to bypassing its later original producer, so that group costs no net extra gate. The second pair `n3,n4` has at most one uncredited completion, `p`; chronological group decompositions use only earlier actual entries. The resulting source-only replay costs at most `G_0+1`, supplies all original source values and the source rows, and contains independent clean values `c,p`. Appending `f=n1+n3` produces three independent clean diagonals within `G_0+2`, so A12 gives `G_0>=10`.

If an original physical clean value `v` is independent of `c`, the three stated cases cover all possibilities: outside `span{c,p}`, equal to `c+p`, or equal to `p`. In the first two cases one appended value supplies the clean triangle plus independent fourth value within `G_0+2`. In the last case `p` is itself original, so completing the second group also has a later bypass credit; the zero-overhead replay can append both `c+p` and `f` within `G_0+2`. The four-diagonal bound then excludes `G_0<=10`. Since the actual clean entry `c` is physically present, the remaining ten-source-gate candidate has physical clean rank exactly one. This is a necessary condition, not a construction or an exclusion of that remaining candidate.
