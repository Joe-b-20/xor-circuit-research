# Independent review: marked-wire duality and repeated labels

Hand review of `../WIRE_DUALITY.md`. No SAT, exhaustive enumeration, circuit search, exact-gate search, or computer-driven case elimination was used. This is not formal verification. The result is separate from, and not needed for, the five-diagonal theorem.

## Verdict

The marked-wire transfer, even-wire separation, and repeated-label no-extra-repair consequence check out for the normalized ten-gate, four-entry consumer specified in the note. The proof relies on the explicit copy/XOR network and does not follow merely by equating the overall linear maps after two independently simplified transpositions.

## Marked-wire transfer

There are eight input ports: four own coordinates and one formal input for each of four physical entry occurrences. With four designated terminal ports and ten XOR nodes, balancing input and output wires in the fully live expanded network gives six binary copy nodes. Reversing wires and swapping XOR/copy nodes is a local transpose and interchanges those counts, so the mirror has six XOR nodes, four ordinary input ports, and eight output ports.

Each formal entry input has exactly one reader. The corresponding mirror trace-output wire therefore branches directly from the copy node dual to that original entry XOR. Deleting that trace port leaves a unary copy; suppressing it retains the marked wire carrying the same entry trace. Reversing the reduced network again restores the original own-projected wiring with precisely the formal-entry inputs removed and the entry XORs bypassed. The value on the bypass wire is the original entry's own mask, since an entry's free operand has zero own part. Local transposition identifies that value with the derivative of the marked mirror wire at the four ordinary mirror outputs. The derivative is for the specific wire, not every use of an equal-valued signal.

The four triples require six ordinary XOR gates. There are exactly six projected non-entry gates, so no projected gate can be zero, a copy of an input, or a duplicate of an earlier value: bypassing it would leave at most five gates. Thus each own fibre has one input or non-entry root and any further physical nodes in that fibre are entries linked to earlier same-own nodes.

## Even-wire separation

In a six-gate triple circuit, the four odd non-input masks are the four distinct designated triples. Every nonzero odd four-bit mask is either one of those triples or an input, so the two other gates are distinct nonzero even values `p,q`. Any XOR reader of either even value must be a designated odd triple: a reader with an odd operand has odd output, while combining distinct `p,q` would create a third nonzero even gate and combining equal values gives zero. In particular `p` and `q` have no common XOR reader.

The full ordinary adjoints `kappa_j` of the four designated triples are linearly independent. Ordering the triple gates topologically makes their terminal-coefficient matrix unitriangular: each has its own terminal contribution and can depend only on later designated outputs. A nonzero-derivative wire in the copy tree of `p` feeds a nonempty subset of `p`'s readers, so its derivative is the XOR of the corresponding `kappa_j`; similarly for `q`. Their reader sets are disjoint, so independence makes any two such nonempty subset sums unequal. Combined with marked-wire transfer, this proves the two different even helper traces cannot come from entries with the same nonzero own mask.

## Repeated-label consequence

With actual labels `u,v,w,w`, where `u,v,w` are independent, the formal target free-mask space maps onto a rank-three space. If its rank were three, injectivity would make the all-four sum of formal target masks zero; all four traces would be even. Three independent even trace signals plus four odd triple signals would require seven mirror gates, contradicting six. Thus the trace rank is four. The nonzero trace-parity vector lies in the one-dimensional label-map kernel generated by the two repeated `w` positions, so exactly those two traces are odd. The `u,v` traces are the two distinct nonzero even helper values. Their marked-wire derivatives are the entries' own masks and must be different by even-wire separation.

The entry edges within each original own fibre form a rooted tree. A same-own exit's two operands differ by the XOR of the entry labels along their tree path. That path can contain at most one of the `u,v` edges, because they have different own masks, and can contain either or both `w` edges. Its value is therefore zero, one actual earlier label, or the sum of two actual earlier labels. Every path label was physically supplied before the exit because its entry lies on an ancestor path to one of the exit operands. The exit can be replayed with at most its original single source-side gate, and all source-side values can be reproduced chronologically without overhead. In the tight joint-consumer setting, A12 then forces at least twelve source-side gates and at least 22 total.

The distinct-label ternary example is consistent with this result; the repeated-label hypothesis is essential to this argument. No general double-transpose identity for arbitrary simplified internal nodes is asserted.
