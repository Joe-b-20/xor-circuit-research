# Independent review: a pointwise-hard ternary exit

Hand review of `../TERNARY_CONSUMER.md` and inspection of its direct fixed-construction verifier. No SAT, exhaustive enumeration, circuit search, exact-gate search, or computer-driven case elimination was used. This is not formal verification.

The ten consumer-bearing gates have the stated rows: direct substitution gives source labels `(a+b+c,a+c,a,a+b)` on the four own triples. These labels span the three independent source inputs and their only relation is the all-four sum. Immediately before the displayed exit, the only source-side values are `a,b,c`; the exit `t+w=a+b+c` is neither an available value nor one XOR of two of them. The consumer's four formal entry traces are exactly `{0,1,2}`, `{0,1}`, `{0}`, and `{3}`, hence have rank four; its actual label relation uses entries 1, 3 and 4. The stated MixColumns diagonal-label substitution produces the four desired row labels.

The example separates a pointwise hard exit from a saving in total source-side work. The source-only repair can compute `a+b` first and then `(a+b)+c`; the original later supplier of `a+b` is now redundant. The more general four-distinct-label argument is also valid: if three earlier available labels are independent and the unique four-label relation has weight three, the missing label must be one of those three related labels, hence the sum of two earlier basis values. If the missing label were already physically available, the rank-three replay would not be in the exceptional exactly-three-values case. Replaying its first later supplier as a wire refunds the one extra gate. Any prior one-XOR realization from arbitrary source values can only improve this conclusion.

The verifier evaluates the one displayed gate list, checks row masks, entry order and traces, early source availability, and the one specified diagonal substitution. It performs no circuit search. The construction does not exhibit a twenty-one-gate joint circuit and does not refute cost-preserving removal for the entire source computation.
