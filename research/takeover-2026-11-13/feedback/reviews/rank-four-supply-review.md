# Independent review: rank-four supply

2026-11-13. I reviewed [RANK_FOUR_SUPPLY.md](../RANK_FOUR_SUPPLY.md), including the prescribed-pair chronological replay, the bypass credit, and all four quotient compositions. I found no material gap. The review uses the stated hand arguments and existing supplier bounds; no circuit search or case elimination was run.

The greedy basis is selected in chronological order of actual entry reads. Hence every entry read before an exit lies in the span of basis elements already read, and the causal span invariant puts the exit in that same span. Once the basis is fixed, its four elements may be partitioned into any two fixed pairs. A demanded pair sum is computed only after both elements have been physically supplied; retaining it costs at most one extra XOR per pair. A source gate with source operands costs at most its original XOR, and a source exit costs at most one XOR of the two available group contributions after any needed pair completions. Completing unused pair sums after replay keeps the total at most `G_0+2` and retains all original source values.

If one prescribed pair sum is an original source value, its physical availability saves a gate in either chronological ordering. If its original production comes first, the pair needs no synthetic computation; if the pair is computed first, the later original occurrence is bypassed. The latter bypass is chronological because the synthetic value is already present. An input or free original value is already available without a paid original production. Thus the replay uses at most `G_0+1` gates in that case. It continues to contain the three original independent clean values and four source rows, so at `G_0<=10` this would contradict A12. Consequently no sum from any prescribed pairing can be an original source value. This is a statement about the original physical values, not about sums that merely lie in their span.

For one nonclean basis element, the three clean elements form a basis of `D`; pairing two yields a physical clean triangle and the third is independent. For two or four nonclean elements, pair like quotient classes: the two sums are distinct nonzero clean values by independence, and neither was an original value, so together with the three original independent clean diagonals they give five distinct physical diagonals. For three nonclean elements, their three pair sums are exactly the nonzero values of a clean plane `W`, and none was original because each is exposed by some pairing. The three original independent clean values therefore lie in the complementary coset `D\W`. Their first two sum to a nonzero member of `W`; the pairing exposing that value supplies their clean triangle, with the third original value independent of it. The cited four- and five-diagonal source bounds contradict the at-most-twelve-gate replay in the respective cases.

The conclusion is correctly conditional on three independent clean diagonal values being physically present in the original source-side values. Rank four of the entry span alone does not supply that physical condition, and the note does not claim it does.
