# Independent review of the four-entry tail closure

2026-11-14. I independently reviewed [FOUR_ENTRY_TAIL_CLOSED.md](../FOUR_ENTRY_TAIL_CLOSED.md), using the four-port extension of [CHAIN_FIBRES.md](../CHAIN_FIBRES.md) already checked in [the chain-fibre review](chain-fibres-review.md). This is machine review, not formal verification or human certification; no circuit search or enumeration was used.

**Verdict:** no material gap. The replay uses at most three additional source gates and supplies the clean triangle and independent diagonal required for the thirteen-gate contradiction.

The core interface is used correctly. Saturation gives an eleven-gate relaxed target core of entry rank four, and the four-entry interface supplies independent actual labels `c,d,n,m`, with `c,d,n+m` an independent clean basis. The outside coloop label `h` is independent of their full span. The proof never treats the core as a complete eleven-consumer joint computation without repairing its source feedback.

The earlier affine-tree reduction forces a useful tail return between different entry components. The own-mask count then gives exactly one two-root fibre. The separately reviewed four-port mirror extension applies: the seven-gate mirror has four odd triples and three independent even helpers with trace classes `{c}`, `{d}`, `{n,m}`. Its tree-cut argument makes the isolated root and an endpoint of the unique entry chain clean in source part. It does not make those source parts physically supplied. The present proof respects that distinction.

The two core helpers `g=c+d` and `e=n+m` are sufficient for every exit between core nodes. Their groups form a direct sum, and the five values `c,d,n,m,h` are independent. Causal entry span therefore gives a unique expanded support using only labels already read. Whenever `g` or `e` is needed, both of its source operands are available. A later clean endpoint is used only to establish an algebraic identity, never as an early source operand.

For a chain in the nonclean class, all within-chain differences lie in `Q=span{n,m}`. The clean isolated-root offset has a fixed `P=span{c,d}` component. If `u` lies on the chain, internal tail returns cost their original XORs and there is at most one return to the isolated root; that return needs at most one extra helper. If `u` is isolated, the same helper `h+P_0` serves every return to the chain. In either orientation, its required `P_0` coefficients occur in the returned value's unique basis support, so the helper is available in time. The corresponding `Q` component has the same guarantee.

For a singleton clean chain, the repeated fibre has three nodes. At least two distinct useful partners force both other nodes to be used. If `u` lies on the chain, its internal return costs one XOR and its unique crossing return costs at most two after core completion. If `u` is isolated, compute the first return with at most two XORs and the second from it by adding the chain label. By the later exit both endpoint nodes have already appeared as return operands, so the entry label linking them has already been read, regardless of which endpoint was produced or returned first. A fibre with no chain has only one possible partner and cannot provide two distinct useful returns.

The extra allowance is thus exactly the two group completions plus at most one tail helper. All other original source operations and feedback returns are preserved by chronological induction. Deferred group helpers may be appended at the end. The resulting source-only circuit has at most `G_0+3<=12` gates and supplies `c,d,c+d,e`, with `e` independent of the clean triangle. Applying the proved thirteen-gate supplier bound is therefore valid.

The scope is appropriately limited to a unique outside entry with a four-entry target core. Other core profiles require their separate closures; six-entry target-live consumers and the full twelve-consumer minimum are not resolved by this note.
