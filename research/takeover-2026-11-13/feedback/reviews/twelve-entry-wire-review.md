# Independent review of the twelve-entry wire reductions

2026-11-14. I independently reviewed [TWELVE_ENTRY_WIRES.md](../TWELVE_ENTRY_WIRES.md), including the five-entry one-root exclusion and its exact repeated-root conclusion. No material gap was found. This is symbolic machine review, not formal verification; no search or enumeration was used.

For `K=tau+6`, explicit fanout expansion has six copies regardless of `tau`, because there are `4+tau` inputs and four outputs. The literal mirror therefore has six XORs, while bypassing the entries gives the minimum six-gate ordinary triple circuit. The original fibre roots and marked-wire identities used in the earlier note are preserved. The odd-run and even-helper separation conclusions depend on this six-gate ordinary budget, not specifically on five entries. The zero-overhead conclusion correctly requires all entry traces to be odd and pairwise distinct.

In the six-entry rank-five interface, trace rank three would make the label map injective on the formal target-row space and force all traces even. Three independent even traces plus four odd triples exceed the six-gate mirror budget. Thus trace rank is four. The quotient map has rank two, its kernel contains the four-dimensional row space, and equality follows. The identities `L=ker Q` and `ker Z=rowspace Q` have the correct domains. The actual-label kernel is one-dimensional and contains the nonzero odd-trace indicator, so that indicator is the unique label relation. Saturated quotient cost supplies the claimed unique-pair and no-new-return properties for every actual nonclean label. Repeated traces are not excluded without an additional hypothesis.

For five entries of actual rank five, the label map is injective. Its formal target-row space has rank three and zero total target sum, so traces are even and span rank three. Four odd triples and three independent even trace values exhaust all seven literal-mirror gates. There is no room for a fourth even trace value or a computed zero; the target-cone hypothesis also precludes an empty reader sum at an entry. The five traces therefore form exactly three independent classes with multiplicities `(3,1,1)` or `(2,2,1)`. Since the trace values are independent, the formal row space consists of all coefficient vectors constant on each class. Applying the injective label map proves that the three class sums are an independent basis of `D`.

Adjacent entries with different traces expose the sum of two of those independent even trace values in a suitable literal mirror. That new nonzero even mask is outside the three required even masks and would need an eighth gate. Hence each connected entry tree has only one trace class. Under the one-root-per-fibre hypothesis every exit path belongs to one class and uses only labels read earlier. In `(2,2,1)`, its value costs at most one XOR. In `(3,1,1)`, the only harder value is the entire triple-class sum; one retained pair helper suffices for every occurrence of that value. When that return occurs it and the two actual singleton labels supply an independent clean diagonal basis, contradicting A12 at the claimed source budget. If it does not occur, zero-overhead replay contradicts the ordinary source-row minimum ten.

The repeated-root conclusion uses a separate ordinary-mask count. Four triple first occurrences and at least two distinct even first occurrences consume six of the seven non-entry gates. Thus failure of the one-root hypothesis leaves exactly one repeated non-entry own occurrence and exactly one two-root fibre; no zero-own non-entry can occur among consumer-bearing gates. These are necessary conditions under the stated target-cone and rank hypotheses, not a general classification of all twelve-gate consumers.

The seven-gate counterexample is correct: `p=x0+x1`, `q=x2+x3`, `t=p+q`, followed by four `t+xi` outputs gives all triples. The two different wires into `t` both affect every output, despite carrying distinct even masks. The common even reader is precisely what six gates cannot support. The example refutes unrestricted reuse of even-wire separation at seven ordinary gates and is not claimed to be a smaller joint circuit.


## Strengthened use of the two-root fibre

I also checked the added requirement that some nonredundant exit must connect the two entry-tree components of the unique two-root fibre. The earlier one-root proof only needs each useful exit's parents to be in the same component: that common root cancels and its path stays within a single trace class. If every cross-component exit repeats an already supplied original source value, those exits can be bypassed chronologically; all remaining exits have the same class-subset form as before. The `(2,2,1)` zero-overhead argument and the `(3,1,1)` one-hard-triple argument therefore give the same contradictions. A cross-component exit producing a previously unavailable source value is necessary. Since every other fibre has one root, it must use the two components of the exceptional fibre. The conclusion correctly does not equate a nonredundant exit with a value that lacks every alternative one-XOR source expression.
