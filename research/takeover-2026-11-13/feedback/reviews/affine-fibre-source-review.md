2026-11-13. I independently reviewed [AFFINE_FIBRE_CLOSURE.md](../AFFINE_FIBRE_CLOSURE.md), including the same-mirror comparison with the outside entry's marked wire, the unique straddling path, and both replay consequences. I found no material gap. The review used symbolic arguments and no circuit search or enumeration. This is machine review, not formal verification or human certification.

The trace premises are correct in both affine-plane profiles. The unique label relation has support on the four plane occurrences, so those four traces are odd and the outside trace is even. When the outside label is clean, the trace relation has support on the four plane traces and the outside trace lies outside their span. When it is nonclean, the four plane traces are independent and the outside trace is their sum. Thus the outside trace cannot equal the sum of two distinct plane traces in either case.

The crucial comparison takes place in one literal transpose. Choose an adjoint accumulation exposing the sum of two adjacent plane traces. That partial-sum wire has ordinary derivative equal to their common original vertex's own mask. The outside entry's marked wire remains available in the same mirror and has derivative equal to its own mask. This remains true if the adjacent-pair vertex is an endpoint of the outside entry: changing the original vertex's binary fanout expansion changes adjoint accumulation, whereas the outside mark comes from the copy dual to its original entry XOR. Both prescribed wire occurrences are retained after deleting formal trace ports. The selected trace sum and the outside trace are distinct nonzero even values, so they must be the two helpers of the six-gate mirror. Equal nonzero own derivatives contradict the independently reviewed even-wire separation theorem.

Consequently no two plane-entry edges share a vertex anywhere in the outside entry's own fibre. There is only one outside entry edge in the whole circuit. Its fibre tree therefore has at most one plane edge incident to each endpoint, and a plane edge cannot lead farther to another plane edge. This proves that there is at most one simple path using the outside edge and two plane edges. Other fibres contain only plane edges. Every path label was read earlier than an exit using its endpoints, so the geometric path statements give chronological source availability rather than merely final-span identities.

For a path avoiding the outside entry, zero or two plane labels give a free value or a pair sum. One or three plane labels give an actual nonclean plane label. The collision theorem excludes a first supply of that value as an exit and makes any later such exit redundant; the argument applies even when that plane label's entry would occur later, because direct later re-entry is also forbidden at this budget. Four plane labels sum to zero. Thus no extra replay work is needed away from the sole straddling path.

The remaining path value is `f=h+w`, with `w=qi+qj` a nonzero clean plane direction. At its exit the two plane labels and `h` are already physically supplied. At most one additional XOR supplies `w`, after which the original exit's one-gate allowance supplies `f`. Retaining `w` handles any repeated use of the same path value; if `w` is an original physical source value, its earlier availability or later original-gate bypass removes the net extra cost. The proof does not assume every geometric candidate is actually expensive.

For clean `h`, positive replay overhead yields the three physical clean values `h,w,f`, with `f=h+w`. An appended difference of two final plane labels supplies a second direction in `W`, independent of that triangle because `h` lies outside the plane-label span. This gives the forbidden four-diagonal supplier within `G0+2` gates. Zero overhead instead contradicts the no-feedback minimum at total at most 21. These alternatives establish the stated closure.
