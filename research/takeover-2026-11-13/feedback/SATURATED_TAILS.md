# Saturated quotient cost restricts gates outside the output cones

**Later continuation:** [six-entry frontier](SIX_ENTRY_FRONTIER.md) excludes the
remaining one-tail twelve-consumer case below 22. The general coloop and core
theorems proved here remain unchanged.

2026-11-14. Structural hand proof. No circuit search, enumeration, or computer-driven elimination. This note distinguishes the full consumer from its relaxed target cones; it does not assume that gates used only for feedback can be discarded.

## A general coloop theorem

Use the model and notation of [saturated quotient projections](SATURATED_QUOTIENT.md). The consumer has `K` physical gates with nonzero ordinary consumer part, actual entry span `U`, and target source space `D subset U`. Put `c=dim(U/D)` and assume

    K=L_D+c.

Form the relaxed target cones by cutting every zero-consumer-part operand into an external source port, then taking the ancestors of the designated consumer outputs. A gate outside these cones may still be essential to the full joint computation through an exit.

**Theorem.** Every consumer-bearing gate outside the relaxed target cones is an entry occurrence belonging to every basis of the actual quotient-label occurrences in `U/D`. In particular there are at most `c` such gates. Each such quotient-label occurrence is a coloop: its image does not lie in the span of the other actual quotient-label occurrences.

Choose any actual entry occurrences whose quotient images form a basis, and let `C` span their full labels. Project the source space along `C` while fixing `D`. Bypass each of the `c` selected entries by redirecting its result to its earlier nonfree operand. Copy the other consumer gates, with projected zero-consumer operands supplied externally. This yields the same targets with at most `K-c=L_D` consumer gates.

An unselected original gate outside the relaxed target cones cannot acquire a target descendant: each bypass redirects an existing edge to an ancestor on an original consumer-bearing path. Such a gate would therefore remain a paid dead gate in this projected construction. Removing it, or any additional simplification already eliminating it, would give fewer than `L_D` gates, a contradiction. Every original outside gate must consequently be among the selected entries. The basis was arbitrary, so it belongs to every quotient basis.

For completeness, a particular occurrence belongs to every vector-space basis drawn from the actual occurrences exactly when its image is outside the span of the other images. If it were in that span, the other occurrences would contain a basis omitting it; if outside, no basis can omit it. Every quotient basis has `c` members, giving the asserted bound.

This theorem is separate from the selected-entry collision forest: that theorem controls all projected equalities, while this one uses optimal target-cone liveness after projection.

**Saturated-core corollary.** If there are `m` outside gates, let `U_Y` span the actual entry labels read inside the relaxed target cones, and put `K_Y=K-m`. Then

    D subset U_Y,    dim(U_Y/D)=c-m,    K_Y=L_D+(c-m).

Indeed, the target source parts lie in the span of the inside entry labels. Every outside gate is an entry, so `U` is generated by `U_Y` and its `m` outside labels. The coloop property makes those `m` quotient images independent modulo `U_Y/D`: any relation with a nonzero outside coefficient would express that outside image using the others. Their removal therefore lowers the quotient rank by exactly `m`. Removing the same `m` gates leaves the stated saturated target core. Source ports in this core are still supplied externally; this conclusion makes no assertion that the outside gates are dispensable in the joint circuit.

## At most one outside gate in a twelve-consumer candidate

Consider a joint two-sheet candidate with total at most 21 and `K=12`, so `G_0<=9`. The [rank-four source floor](RANK_FOUR_SOURCE_FLOOR.md) forces `dim U=5`, hence `c=2`, and the consumer saturates quotient cost because `L_D=10`. The [full-diagonal supply bound](FULL_DIAGONAL_SUPPLY.md) gives

    m_D=|A intersect (D minus {0})|<=2,

where `A` is the original physical source-value set.

The general theorem permits at most two outside gates. If there were two, the relaxed target cones would contain exactly ten consumer gates. Let `U_Y` span the actual labels read inside those cones. Their targets still require `D subset U_Y`, and entry deletion applied to this ten-gate consumer gives `dim(U_Y/D)=0`. Thus its actual labels are clean and span `D`. At least three distinct nonzero clean values would be original physical source values, contradicting `m_D<=2`.

Therefore **at most one consumer-bearing gate can lie outside the relaxed target cones**. The statement is global; subsequent target-live classifications must retain this one-tail possibility.

If that unique outside gate exists, the saturated-core corollary gives an eleven-gate relaxed target core with `dim U_Y=4` and quotient rank one. This is a constraint on its interface, not a reduction of the full joint circuit to the already closed eleven-consumer budget.

## A surviving outside entry must support two distinct returns

Work with a reduced candidate: bypass zero and duplicate full values and remove unused gates. If normalization lowers the consumer budget to at most eleven, the [already closed budgets](ELEVEN_CONSUMER_CLOSED.md) exclude a total below 22. Thus it is enough to consider a reduced twelve-consumer candidate.

Suppose its unique outside gate is `v=u+h`, where `h` is its zero-consumer entry label. Every reader of `v` must be a zero-consumer exit. A consumer-bearing reader would either give another outside gate or put `v` inside a target cone. The gate is not itself a designated consumer output.

If `v` has no reader it can be removed, contrary to reduction. Suppose it has only one exit reader `f=v+w`. Since `f` is zero-consumer, `u` and `w` have the same ordinary consumer part. Reassociate these two gates as

    g=u+w,        f=g+h.

Both new gates are zero-consumer. All their operands are available by the original exit time: `u,h` precede `v`, and `w` precedes `f`. The original value `f` and every other later use are preserved. Zero or already available `g` only saves further gates.

The reassociation removes one consumer gate, replaces one original source gate by at most two, and does not increase the total. There is no hidden dependency through `v` in `w`: its only reader is `f`, so such a dependency would give a directed cycle. The new circuit has at most eleven consumer gates below 22, contradicting the established bounds. Consequently a surviving unique outside entry must have at least two exit readers. In a reduced circuit these return distinct, nonzero, previously unavailable source values; redundant returns could have been bypassed first.

This necessary condition does not eliminate the tail. Multiple returns can share the outside entry, and their separate reassociations may cost more than deleting that entry saves. Controlling that sharing remains part of the twelve-consumer problem.
