# A source triangle and a separated pair admit one-helper replay

2026-11-14. Structural hand proof using actual triangle completion, marked even-wire separation, and chronological replay. No circuit search or trace enumeration.

## A general replay condition

Suppose every consumer own-mask fibre is an entry tree. Assume the actual entry labels consist of a source triangle

    {a,b,c},       c=a+b,

two further labels `k,l`, and a label `d`. Suppose no fibre contains a `d` entry together with a `k` or `l` entry. Then every original source value has a source-only replay with at most `G_0+1` gates, and the replay can also supply `e=k+l`.

Maintain the span of the triangle values already physically available. Once two are available, the third can be completed if needed. This has no net extra cost: all three values are original physical source values, so an early completion either reuses an already available value or advances its later original first producer, which can be bypassed. A free triangle value is available initially.

Retain the one additional helper `e=k+l` when first needed. It costs at most one extra gate. Append it after replay if it has not been needed earlier.

Every exit is the sum of the actual labels on its entry-tree path. By the fibre separation hypothesis, this is either a triangle value plus one value from

    {0,k,l,e},

or a triangle value plus `d` or zero. The triangle component may be zero. After the maintained completions, the exit therefore uses at most its original one source XOR.

This construction is chronological. Every path label was read before the exit. The triangle component lies in the span of triangle labels on that path, so its completion uses already supplied values. The helper `e` is needed only when both `k,l` occur, and both have then been read. Ordinary source gates and unrelated repetitions are copied or bypassed in order. Thus all original source values are preserved, with at most the one extra value `e` beyond the credited triangle completion.

## A mixed pair-of-pairs trace profile

Consider a target-live twelve-consumer circuit with six entry occurrences, actual label rank five, and two repeated trace pairs. Suppose their actual-label relation has three terms. The repeated-trace relations span the two-dimensional trace kernel, so the four labels of the pairs occupy two distinct nonzero quotient classes and the two singleton labels are clean.

The three-term relation must contain exactly one whole repeated pair and one singleton, because it is the indicator of odd trace positions. Name the first pair `a,b`, its singleton `c=a+b`, the other pair `k,l`, and the remaining singleton `d`. Thus the `a,b` trace and the `c` trace are odd, while the `k,l` trace and the `d` trace are the two distinct even helpers. The four trace values are independent.

The six-gate ordinary minimum makes every own-mask fibre one entry tree. Even-wire separation puts the `d` entry in a different fibre from either `k` or `l`. The general replay condition therefore applies.

The target source space is generated by the four trace-class sums. In this profile it is

    D=span{c,d,e},       e=k+l,

and these three clean values are independent. One way to see the independence is to use the rank-five actual label map: its only relation is `a+b+c=0`, so no relation among `c,d,k+l` is possible.

The source-only replay supplies the four source rows and this diagonal basis within `G_0+1` gates. By [A12](../augmented/PROOF.md),

    G_0+1>=12,       hence G_0>=11.

In particular this profile cannot occur in a twelve-consumer joint circuit of total at most 21. The argument gives a local source lower bound for this specified profile, not an attainment claim or a conclusion for the other repeated-pair profiles.
