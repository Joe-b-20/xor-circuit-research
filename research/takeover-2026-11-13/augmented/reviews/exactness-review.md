# Independent review of the augmented, joint, and block-monotone bounds

2026-11-13. Machine proof review. The proofs and explicit constructions were checked by hand, without circuit search or family checks.

## Augmented minimum

Assume an eleven-gate circuit computes the four first-sheet rows and three independent even diagonals. The earlier augmented lower bound of eleven applies without a restriction on cancellation. Thus no zero, duplicate, wholly free, or dead paid gate can be removed: removal would give at most ten gates for the same targets. Every paid gate has nonzero own part. Since all target free parts are even, an odd free label could be killed by projecting the free space onto its even subspace along the line spanned by that label. The entry using the label would disappear, leaving the same targets in at most ten gates. This verifies parity confinement for the actual labels.

At least three entries are needed to span the row free parts. The four distinct own triples and three independent own masks of the diagonal outputs are distinct non-input own masks. Each needs a first-occurrence gate, and an entry repeats the own part of its nonfree operand. Thus there are at most four entries.

The [three-entry proof](../THREE_ENTRIES.md) excludes all cases. The relation-rank bound gives \(c+r\le4\) for \(r=\dim\rho(\operatorname{span}H)\). The named residues occupy every odd residue and edges stay in residue cosets, so \(c\ge2^{3-r}\). This forces \(r\ge2\) and at most two odd helpers. With zero helpers the named pairs and even-only allowance give at most six equations. With one helper the doubled colours share the same formal dependence and give at most five odd-pair dimensions. The even-only subspace contributes at most one more. With two helpers the five even values are independent and the two residue planes must each be connected. Projecting onto residues requires at least three nonclean edges per plane. Both nonclean colours would then attain three pairs, impossible by uniqueness of each helper's three-element named expansion. All three cases fall short of eight non-entry equations.

## Four entries and the three-helper equality case

With four entries, the seven required first occurrences exhaust the non-entry gates. The only even own masks are the three independent diagonal own masks. A zero-sum relation on three physical even values would project to three occurrences from this basis, which cannot sum to zero. This excludes every even-only relation, including when the full even values are dependent. Exactly three physical even values are clean, one for each of the three own masks.

In the [four-entry proof](../FOUR_ENTRIES.md), \(c+r\le5\) excludes \(r=0\) and leaves at most three helpers. For zero or one helper, the same named-pair and common formal dependence arguments bound the rank below seven. With two helpers, at most two clean pairs exist: distinct helper residues contribute at most one each, and equal residues give three possible pairs on three odd nodes whose three distinct sums would be dependent if all were clean. If a nonclean colour has three pairs, the two-helper saturation lemma gives the other nonclean colour at most two. When it has two, its alternating four-cycle with the saturated colour is a dependence in formal node coordinates. This uses no independence of the full even set. The total rank is at most six.

With three helpers the four even values consist of three independent clean values \(h_1,h_2,h_3\) and one nonclean value \(k\). They are independent and have residue rank one. The eight named values occupy four residue pairs modulo \(\langle\rho(k)\rangle\). Seven non-entry equations on eleven odd nodes force these four residue-pair classes to be connected, since the incidence rank is at most eleven minus the component count. Every class requires a \(k\)-edge. The translation by \(k\) is a matching with at most one wholly named edge and at most three further edges incident to helpers. Thus there are exactly four such edges, with one named edge and three helper-to-named edges. They occupy different classes. Each of the three helper classes contains its two named points and one helper; the \(k\)-edge meets one of the named points, so the other must connect by a clean edge to the helper. The four named pair sums are consequently \(k,k+h_i,k+h_j,k+h_m\). Named-pair uniqueness forces the three indices to be distinct. The four pairs partition all eight named nodes. Their sums add to zero by the unique full named relation, giving \(h_1+h_2+h_3=0\). This contradicts independence. The argument counts all available relations and remains valid if the actual gates use a proper subset.

This excludes four entries and completes the eleven-gate exclusion. The [specified twelve-gate construction](../../compatibility/DIAGONALS.md) is valid and uses the independent masks \(01,23,12\). Its own and free masks verify directly from the displayed operands. Thus the minimum to obtain the four rows and any three independent even diagonals is exactly 12. This equality does not say that every prescribed independent diagonal basis has a twelve-gate realization.

## Exact joint cost under no feedback

Consider the two-sheet problem with \(a\) free, own block \(b\) for the first sheet and \(b'\) for the second. Assume no first-sheet output ancestor carries \(b'\). Discard wholly free gates if present. Let \(C_0\) be the first-sheet gate cone. It has at least ten gates, all zero on \(b'\). The \(b'\)-bearing gates also number at least ten by the single-sheet bound with all other coordinates free.

If the total were at most 21 and \(|C_0|=10\), [J10](../../crossing/ODD_CLOSURE.md) already gives a contradiction, including when an extra supplier is an exit. Otherwise the only budget is eleven gates in \(C_0\), ten \(b'\)-bearing gates, and no additional zero-\(b'\) gate. The ten-gate consumer's entry labels all lie in \(D\) by the entry-deletion bound for the four triples and their required label space. They must span \(D\). Each nonzero clean label is a physical gate value in \(C_0\), since ordinary coordinate inputs and wholly free masks cannot be nonzero diagonals. Thus \(C_0\) would supply the four rows and three independent diagonals in eleven gates, which the augmented minimum excludes. The known 22-gate construction attains the bound and is event-free, so the no-feedback joint minimum and the block-monotone joint minimum are both 22.

## Disjoint counting in block-monotone MixColumns

The local result can be used without assuming additivity. For a block-monotone circuit, let \(H\) be the union of the sheet-0 and sheet-1 cones after excluding gates supported on block 7 alone and zero. It is closed under operands up to free block-7 masks and input coordinates. Its support is contained in \(\{0,1,7\}\). Every ancestor of a sheet-0 output avoids block 1. Hence \(|H|\ge22\) by the exact joint result.

For \(j=2,4,5,6,7\), count the ordinary sheet-\(j\) charged family, consisting of gates nonzero on block \(j\) in its output cones. These five families each have at least ten gates. For sheet 3 include all its charged gates and also its ancestors meeting blocks 2 and 7. The three-term local lemma gives at least thirteen in this family. These six families are pairwise disjoint: the ordinary charges cannot overlap without reciprocal own-block window incidence, and only the sheet-3 window contains both blocks 2 and 7.

They are also disjoint from \(H\). The intersections of \(\{0,1,7\}\) with the windows of sheets \(2,3,4,5,6,7\) are respectively \(\{1\},\{7\},\{7\},\varnothing,\varnothing,\{7\}\). The own-block conditions exclude an overlap for sheets 2 through 6, including the extra sheet-3 family because it requires block 2. Any sheet-7 overlap would be supported on block 7 alone and is already excluded from \(H\). Therefore the separate sets count at least \(22+13+5\cdot10=85\) physical gates. This improves the lower bound for block-monotone circuits, including event-free circuits. The unrestricted MixColumns interval remains \(75\le L\le88\).

## Independent baseline for normalization and parity

The consolidated proof can obtain the eleven-gate preliminary bound without relying on the earlier augmented argument. Seven distinct own first occurrences and at least three entries imply at least ten gates. If there were exactly ten, then there would be exactly three entries. Their actual labels span the three-dimensional even space of the free block, so all free parts are even. In the notation above, \(\dim V=3+r\) and the general relation-rank inequality gives \(c+r\le\tau+1=4\). The distinct named residues give \(c\ge2^{3-r}\). Thus \(r\ge2\), there are at least five even gates, and there is at most one odd helper.

With no helper there are six even values and at least three clean ones. At most three odd-pair relations are available, one for each possible nonclean value. The even-only gate count is at most \(6-\dim V\le1\), for a total of at most four non-entry gates. With one helper there are five even values and \(\dim V=5\), so the values are independent and exactly three are clean. Across the clean values there is at most one pair involving the lone helper and its unique same-residue named point. Each of the two nonclean values has at most two pairs. There are no even-only equations, so at most five non-entry gates are possible. Both contradict the seven needed. This proves the preliminary eleven-gate bound from the same rank framework. An actually read odd free label at eleven could then be projected away with its entry gate, contradicting that bound and proving parity confinement.

## Line audit of the consolidated proof

The saved [PROOF](../PROOF.md) follows a noncircular order. Normalize a minimum circuit for all seven outputs; count seven non-input own first occurrences and at least three entries; deduce even free labels at ten gates; use the relation bound to exclude ten; then project away an odd label to prove parity confinement at eleven. The three- and four-entry arguments use this established confinement. They do not require J10, the ten-gate producer closure, or the additional row-ancestor property of an augmented eleven-gate candidate. The historical parity references in the branch notes can be replaced by this independently established step without affecting the proof.

The relation inequality itself permits all cancellations: each non-entry equation has a latest-result pivot, and a formal relation with zero odd incidence evaluates to zero on the physical even values. The residue count uses the eight actual named masks and their unique all-eight relation. The proof of the ten-gate baseline needs only the number of named pairs and the allowance for even-only production. The eleven-gate exclusions then cover every possible entry count after normalization. The attainment statement is existential over the independent basis, while the lower bound holds for every basis.
