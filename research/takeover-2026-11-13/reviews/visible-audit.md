# Independent review: visible-event repair

Review of `visible/VISIBLE.md` and the relevant parts of `repair/REPAIR.md`, against `RECORD.md` §§7, 9, 10, 17, `tenth-gate/PROOF.md`, `joint01/REPORT.md`, and the joint01 reviews. This is a mathematical review, not formal verification. No SAT, enumeration, exact circuit search, or scripts were run.

## Conclusions

The global visible repair, its support and credit lemmas, and the accounting that yields Theorem V withstand the checks below. In particular, subject to the stated 83-gate charged-family ingredient, one visible event forces at least 80 gates and two force at least 76. Subject also to the stated 84-gate local ingredient, two visible events force at least 77. These are structural restrictions and do not establish a new unrestricted lower bound above 75.

There is one overstatement in §6.3: different `J(f)` sets do not by themselves prevent two event reconstruction chains from sharing a gate while leaving its charged-family memberships unchanged. This does not affect Theorem V, which explicitly uses separate chains and does not rely on the negative claim.

The 84 form depends on a substantially longer case analysis for the joint local 21-gate theorem. I traced the logical interface and the decomposition, including the cited corrections, but did not independently certify every case in that underlying analysis. Accordingly the 84-form consequences are conditional on that theorem, not independently re-proved here. The 83 form is independent of that 21-gate theorem.

## Visible repair and Theorem V

1. Every retained non-event gate reads only an input, an earlier retained gate, or a visible event: its own event-free continuation extends backward through any non-event operand. Designated outputs are likewise retained, visible, or inputs. Therefore independent event-free gadgets for visible values followed by the original retained gates compute the same outputs and have `|R| + K` gates. The operands of every retained gate have their original values, so it cannot become an event. This proves the repair inequality, including the physical-gate credit `|E \\ F| + |Q|`.
2. On an event-free path block supports can only grow; hence a visible event's support is contained in every window in `J(f)`. The displayed windows have no pair of mutually included own blocks. Consequently its support meets at most one own block of sheets in `J(f)`. The pair `{2,7}` occurs in only `W_3`. These facts give exactly the two kinds of charged chain gates described in §5.2.
3. Keep each rebuilding chain physically separate, even if two gate values coincide, and designate the prescribed output nodes. The output descendants of a chain gate are exactly those reached event-free from its event. Thus its family memberships follow from the support of that gate and `J(f)`, including the exceptional `{2,7}` rule for family 3. Separate physical chains are important: sharing nodes can change the descendant sets and must be re-accounted.
4. The displayed `in(f)` counts follow from the chain order. For `t=0` the off-own chain contributes no family gate, and the own-block piece plus the final combination contribute `w_A` if there is an off part, or `w_A-1` otherwise. For `t=1`, the block-3 piece plus the one join reaching both blocks 2 and 7 contributes `w_3+1` if `w_3>0`, otherwise one. In particular `A=empty, t=1` gives `in=1` and `kappa=0`, not -1.
5. The families are disjoint and have minimum total 83 in the repaired circuit, assuming the 10-gate and three-term-sheet ingredients. The rebuilt gates outside all families are actual, distinct physical gates, even if their values duplicate other nodes. Therefore `|R| + sum in(f) >= 83`, and rewriting `G=|R|+|F|+|E\\F|+|Q|` gives Theorem V. This also proves the per-family statement.
6. The ten-gate hand proof's key count is sound: formal free operands give at least three entry traces spanning the even subspace; transposition gives a four-input circuit with `K-tau` gates computing four distinct odd triple sums and containing those traces. Four entries plus six mirror gates cost ten; with exactly three entries the three independent even traces require three further gates in addition to the four triple sums. The three-term-sheet version uses the same reasoning with at least three free-operand traces from each side. These arguments do not require enumeration.

## Credits and quantitative consequences

If a cancelled block `c` is nonadjacent to a block `b` of a visible event's value, the operand carrying `b` also carries `c`, so its support lies in no output window. It cannot be an input and belongs to the hidden part. This is one physical-gate credit, not one credit per event. The sole-event version is `Q >= 1` because the event is visible in a live circuit.

For `kappa=4`, the special family-3 case has weight four on block 3 and nonzero pieces on both 2 and 7, hence support `W_3` and at least one hidden credit. Otherwise `kappa<=3`. This proves the one-visible-event bound `G>=80`. For two visible events, if both have `kappa=4` at least one hidden credit is available, giving `83-8+1=76`; all other pairs give at least 76 directly or better. For the 84 form, when both have `kappa=4` neither is a sheet-1 borrower, so the same shared credit gives `84-8+1=77`; if only one has `kappa=4`, the 83 form gives at least `83-7+1=77`; if neither does, it gives at least `83-6=77`.

The 84 slack statement requires more than the bare event-free inequality `G' >= 84`. It needs the explicit disjoint decomposition `F_2+F_5+F_6`, `L_01`, `F_7+F_3+F_4`, and the fact that `L_01=(F_0 union F_1) disjoint union X`. With the joint local theorem applied to all of `L_01`, this gives `G' >= 84 + |U\\X|`; it justifies charging chain gates in `X` separately. The reconstruction for sheet-1 borrowers gives the stated `x=(w_0-1)+[w_7>0]`, taking the block-0 piece first in the off-own construction.

## Correction to the discussion of sharing

Section 6.3 says visible events with different `J(f)` cannot share chain gates without changing their family memberships. For example, an event visible only to sheet 0 can have support in `{0,7}`, while another visible only to sheet 3 can have support in `{3,7}`. If both values use the same two block-7 inputs, their chains may share the gate adding those inputs. Before sharing, that gate is outside every charged family in each chain. Afterwards its descendants reach sheets 0 and 3, but it remains outside family 0 (no block 0) and outside family 3 (neither block 3 nor block 2); it joins no other family. Thus its memberships do not change. More generally, sharing requires checking the combined descendant sets; some sharing is possible without changing them. The separate-chain proof and all bounds above remain valid.

## Projection repair

The first-event projection construction also checks out at the level of its stated hypotheses. Zeroing complete blocks cannot create an event at an originally non-event gate, and after omitting zero/copy nodes it produces the event value. Dominated cancelled-block carriers die; their readers on paths to the first event remain within its event-free ancestry, so supports grow along those paths. A shared projected value already available later can be paid for by deleting that later duplicate, provided it is distinct from the repaired event; this explains the definition excluding `e` itself. Normalization between sequential repairs is required for reapplying the first-event and liveness assumptions. The resulting bound `sigma_e <= s_e + delta_e - 1` is an upper bound on repair overhead, not generally an exact cost.
