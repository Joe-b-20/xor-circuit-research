# Review of the projection-tree normal form

2026-11-13. Independent machine proof review of [PROJECTION_TREE](../PROJECTION_TREE.md) and the relevant count in [RANK_TWO](../RANK_TWO.md). No circuit search or family check was run.

## Projected circuit

Three independent even own directions need at least three distinct nonzero even first occurrences. Four distinct triple masks need four more. Each of the at least three entries repeats an own mask. At size ten these sets exhaust the physical gates, giving exactly seven non-entry first occurrences and three entries. The entry labels span the even free space and are independent. Setting the free part to zero and bypassing entries retains precisely the seven first occurrences, because their projected masks are distinct non-input values. The projected circuit has the four units, four triples, and three independent even masks as its nonzero values.

The eight odd own masks are one affine space over the three-dimensional even subspace. The graph allowing additions of the three even basis values is its cube graph. Each projected gate contributes one odd pair labelled by its even sum. A gate cannot add two even values: two different basis elements sum to an absent mask, and equal operands sum to zero. A pair cannot be used twice because the latest of its two odd nodes and its even node is the only possible result of that equation.

A cycle in the selected edges would sum to zero in actual endpoint differences. Independent even directions would force every colour to occur an even number of times. Summing the corresponding formal equations would then give zero on both odd and even node coordinates. This contradicts the independent latest-result pivots of the gate equations. Thus seven selected edges on eight vertices form a tree, with three edges producing even values and four producing odd values.

## Physical lift

There are exactly four odd first occurrences among the gates. Therefore the number of other odd gates is the number of odd entries, equal to the number \(A\) of odd non-output gates. This is a counting identity. A designated output may itself be an entry, and an odd non-output gate may be the first gate of a triple fibre. The normal form does not require the two sets of nodes to coincide.

The span of the physical even values is generated by the three even fibre roots and the \(3-A\) labels of even entries. Each label is the difference of its entry and its same-fibre predecessor. Conversely each even value is the sum of its root and the labels along its entry path. The three roots have independent own projections and the pure-free entry labels are independent, so this span has rank \(3+(3-A)=e\). The physical even values are independent even when several have the same own mask.

The seven original non-entry equations thus select seven distinct odd-pair edges. A cycle would again have zero actual sum and hence every physical even colour occurring evenly. The resulting formal dependence is impossible. The odd graph has \(8+A\) vertices and seven edges, so the forest has \(A+1\) components. Adding the \(A\) odd-entry edges connects each own fibre into its rooted tree. Contracting all such fibre trees yields the projected cube tree, so the expanded graph is connected. It has one fewer edge than vertex and is a tree.

The rank-two note uses \(A\) only as a count of odd non-output gates. Its three-even-gate exclusion uses the number of odd nodes and the available relation rank, independent of which odd nodes are entry outputs. The known control with three pure even basis gates has two odd entries and one even entry, consistent with the stated normal form. The result classifies the stated projection profile and does not exclude every rank-two supplier.
