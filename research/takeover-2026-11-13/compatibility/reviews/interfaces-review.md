# Independent review of the producer coset cap

2026-11-13. Machine proof review of [INTERFACES](../INTERFACES.md). No circuit or configuration search was run.

## Reduction and parity

A ten-gate sheet must already be reduced and live with respect to its four targets. Deleting a duplicate, a gate with a free value, or an unused gate would leave a circuit below the local ten-gate bound. Every counted gate therefore has nonzero own part and a distinct non-input value. The transpose count gives three or four entries. The target free parts span the even space of the free block, which proves parity confinement directly with three entries. With four entries, an odd label would force the four labels to be independent and the formal target label rows to have rank three and total sum zero. Their even traces would need at least three gate masks beyond the four triple sums, contradicting the six-gate transpose. This verifies parity confinement for computed values. The supplied free values themselves are unrestricted.

Projection onto the own part is injective on a clean-diagonal coset. Such a coset contains at most one wholly free value. Thus a coset with odd free-part parity cannot contain five available values. A five-value coset with even own-part parity would require at least four nonzero even own masks as well as the four odd triple masks. Their first occurrences need eight gates. Entries copy the own mask of their other operand and cannot be first occurrences, and at least three entries are required. This excludes the even case.

## Odd cosets and relation capacity

The residue map \((\beta,\alpha)\mapsto\beta+\alpha\), restricted to even free-part parity, has kernel equal to the clean diagonal. The four own units and four required targets have eight distinct odd residues, the units and triples. These eight named values also have precisely one nonzero relation, their total sum. A relation must include either none or all of the target free parts by the adjacent-pair relation, and all four targets sum to the pure own parity. Consequently no nonzero value can be the sum of two different unordered pairs of distinct named values.

Five available values in an odd coset would include at least four non-output odd gates. Along with the four required odd outputs they leave at most two even gates. At least two even gates are necessary: with just one nonzero even projected mask, its first occurrence must come from two distinct own units, giving a pair \(p\). Later odd projected masks stay in the orbits \(\{e_i,e_i+p\}\), since an odd XOR step either adds \(p\) or leaves the projection unchanged through an entry. Only two triples lie in those orbits. This phrasing allows an odd input mask to recur after two additions of \(p\), so it strengthens the wording of the initial draft. With no nonzero even projected mask, no triple can be formed.

It follows that there are exactly two physical even gates and exactly four non-output odd gates. The four are all in the five-value coset, which also contains its unique named value. Every other odd residue is occupied by at most one named value. For a fixed physical even value \(h\) of residue zero, odd pairs summing to it must stay within a residue. Only the five-value coset permits such pairs, and translation by nonzero \(h\) allows at most two disjoint pairs there. For nonzero residue, no pair lies entirely within the five-value coset. A pair crossing it must use the unique named value at the appropriate outside residue, which fixes the other endpoint. Outside-only pairs are named pairs and there is at most one. This proves the capacity of two for either even value.

Every non-entry gate yields one of these odd-pair relations. No ordinary input has even own parity. A gate using two physical even operands would require three distinct physical even values, including its result, or else have zero own part. For an even result the operands are therefore both odd. For an odd result, one operand is odd and the other is a physical even value, so rearranging gives the corresponding odd pair. Because each value has a unique node, the latest of the three nodes in a relation is the only one that can have been computed from the other two. Hence the assignment of non-entry gates to these relations is injective. Four available relations contradict the at least six non-entry gates. It is irrelevant whether one of the even gates is itself an entry.

## Joint-sheet application

With the recorded no-feedback hypothesis, the ten first-side gates in a \((10,11)\) split all belong to the first-sheet computation. Every entry operand on the second side comes from those gates, an ordinary first-side input, or a free vector. The all-nonclean eleven-gate theorem requires five distinct such operands in one nonzero diagonal coset. Distinctness follows either from its sole four-label relation or from the occurrence-deletion bound, which would remove two equal nonclean entries. The coset cap excludes that interface, so the second side must use a clean label. It does not exclude mixed-label interfaces or the \((11,10)\) split. No new total gate bound follows by itself.

## Rank-one clean supply

The additional rank-one result holds. An all-clean consumer would need three independent clean labels. With a nonclean label, the quotient bound forces total label rank four. The four-entry branch requires two independent clean labels, so only the five-entry branch remains. Two nonclean occurrences together with a clean span of rank at most one give rank at most three. With three nonclean occurrences, the other two labels are equal and nonzero, since the deletion certificate excludes a zero label in a tight nonclean interface. The unique label relation therefore consists of these two clean positions, and the parity formula makes all three nonclean traces even. The unique trace relation has support on those three nonclean positions, so these traces are distinct and nonzero. A six-gate transpose has room for at most two such even masks. Five nonclean labels are excluded by the coset cap. This leaves four distinct nonclean values in one coset and one nonzero clean value. The argument depends on the rank of the actually supplied clean values, and remains necessary even when some available values cannot be read early enough.

For the specified producer in [JOINT01](../../row-cost/JOINT01.md), the residues of \(p,q,u,v,w,d\) are respectively \(s_0,s_3,t,t,E_1,0\), where \(t\) is the four-coordinate parity. The four output residues are \(E_{c+1}\). Only \(d\) is a nonzero clean value. A free mask with even parity is unique at its residue, and an odd free mask is in a separate coset with no computed value. Thus the largest available coset for this producer has size three, attained at \(t\) by \(u,v\) and the pure free parity. The rank-one result excludes any eleven-gate second-side interface with this fixed producer, so its joint total under the no-feedback hypothesis is at least 22. This does not assert the same restriction for every ten-gate producer.

## Even free labels at the joint minimum

The general deletion argument also applies to a full 21-gate joint circuit under the no-feedback hypothesis. Treat the \(b,b'\) coordinates as ordinary and the whole \(a\) block as free. The required free parts span the even subspace of \(a\). An actually read odd \(a\)-only label can be killed by projecting along its span onto that even subspace. Its entry gate then becomes a wire and can be deleted. The ordinary parts on both \(b,b'\) remain unchanged. Bypassing an entry only redirects to its original nonfree operand, so any new path to a first-sheet output follows an original ancestor path. The no-feedback condition survives. The result would have at most 20 gates for the same joint targets, contradicting the known 21 bound under that condition. Thus every free label actually read by such a circuit is even. The argument does not remove the odd vectors from the model's nominal free set.
