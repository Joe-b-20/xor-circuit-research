## 4. Worker-count hypothesis: many more trajectories on an oversubscribed CPU

Setup [measured]: repo v8, AES MixColumns uncapped from fresh roots, no sharing, 20 threads, 60 min
per arm, arms run sequentially, the same campaign seed per trial (the 20 arm's roots are the first
20 of the 100 / 300 arm's), independent RNG streams per worker (seed + ordinal x stride), restart
policy expressed in PROPOSALS (stall 1.8 M = v8's 900 s at full speed, incumbent factor 2, root
budget off) so oversubscription does not change it. Known reachable target: 88. Memory and load
sampled every 30 s. Throughput cost curve (8 min, hunt-A config): 20 workers 28,100 proposals/s;
100: 25,500 (-10 %); 300: 13,700 (-47 %); ~27-30 MB RSS per worker.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| t1 / 20 | 31,400 | 35 (15) | 23 | 77 | 632 | 94 (222) | 0.7 GB |
| t1 / 300 | 22,700 | 300 (0) | 54 | 673 | 2,316 | 93 (234) | 8.9 GB |
| t2 / 20 | 32,400 | 38 (18) | 30 | 60 | 470 | 226 (514) | 0.7 GB |
| t2 / 100 | 30,900 | 100 (0) | 54 | 304 | 1,806 | 163 (398) | 3.1 GB |
| t2 / 300 | 26,900 | 300 (0) | 59 | 491 | 1,893 | 103 (250) | 9.0 GB |

(Signatures: the cheap plateau identity of hunt/sig.py on the g88 records, an upper bound on
distinct exact plateaus; 0 signatures shared between arms except 2 between t2/20 and t2/100.)

Conclusion: **unhelpful under the tested conditions.** Spreading the same compute over 100 or 300
persistent trajectories (a) delayed the first arrival at the target 5x (100) and 8-9x (300) and the
median trajectory 3.5-4x, (b) did not increase the number of distinct 88 plateaus discovered per
wall-hour (equal at 300 in trial 1; fewer at 100 and 300 in trial 2), and (c) cost 5 % (100) to
17-28 % (300) of aggregate throughput, with memory fine. The one quantity that rose, arrivals at 88
(about 2x), is the restart-policy effect of F6 in disguise: short trajectories never reach the
post-arrival stall period, which the 20-worker arm spends hopping between 88 plateaus through 89,
and those hops are where its larger signature count comes from. Same-start divergence does not
convert into more independent chances per unit of compute. Recommendation: one trajectory per
thread; if more independent descents per hour are wanted, shorten the stall policy (F6) rather
than oversubscribe. Not tested: harder targets (nothing reachable below 88 is known), sharing, and
oversubscription at 2-3x (40-60 workers), which the cost curve says would be nearly free in
throughput but whose benefit the 100-arm result does not suggest.

## 5. The search change tested: the "foundation restart" source

Mechanism targeted (from the 2026-11-02 foundations study [measured there]): on MixColumns at
depth cap 4 the level-1 set (weight-2 values) largely decides the reachable gate count; the walk
changes level 1 one pair at a time and cannot cross the steep landscape between foundations (one
pair swapped: completions 92-93; four: 97-102), while a good foundation held fixed is re-completed
in minutes. The change (commit 8e544ba on `phase-2026-11-09`, default off, default path
byte-identical, 371 tests): a restart source that keeps the worker best's or an archive elite's
level-1 set, swaps `swap_pairs` of its pairs for random useful pairs, builds a restricted root over
the set and runs the new chain pinned to it for `pin_proposals` proposals, then unpins. It keeps
the configuration that made the elite good and discards the unproductive upper walk, instead of a
fresh root. Design data: the foundations study. Evaluation data: three benchmarks below (the 89@4's
first layer was never supplied; swaps were random; settings swap_pairs 2, pin 150,000 proposals).

| benchmark (both arms share every timer) | baseline arm | foundation arm | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 hard diagnostic: cap-4 MixColumns from 12 recorded 90@4 starts with different first layers (5-20 of the 89's 26 pairs), 1 worker each, 30 min, stall 300 s | 0 of 12 reached 89; 43 restarts (fresh / elite / best); finished chain bests 90-96 (20 at 90) | 0 of 12 reached 89; 33 foundation restarts (10 constructor failures fell back), 31 pinned phases ending at 91-101 gates (median 94-95); finished chain bests 90-93 (17 at 90) | **falsified** at this budget: random two-pair perturbations plus pinned re-completion reproduce the +3 barrier of the foundations study and never land on a better foundation |
| B2 calibration: uncapped MixColumns from fresh roots, 6 workers x 40 min, v8 timers, w_fresh 1 vs w_fresh 0.5 / w_foundation 0.5 | 7 of 10 chains at 88 (median 180 s from root); 62 signatures / 174 records | 6 of 9 chains at 88 (median 420 s); 39 / 108; only 1 foundation restart fired (unpinned at 92, no 88) | **uninformative**: v8's 1,800 s incumbent stall allows 3-4 restarts per arm in 40 min |
| B3 transfer: InvMixColumns cap 5 from our 168@5, 6 workers x 40 min, stall 300 s | frontier 129@5; 2 chains <= 130; finished bests 129-159 | frontier 129@5; 1 chain <= 130; 10 foundation restarts, pinned phases end at 177-199 gates; finished bests 129-155 | **no benefit, slight cost**: the level-1-restricted root is a poor start for InvMixColumns (weight-19 outputs) and the pinned phase spends the restart far above the frontier |

No 87, no 88@4, no InvMC below 129@5 in any arm. Understanding gained: (1) the barrier between
foundations is real for the engine as well as for the prototype: re-completion from a two-pair
perturbation of a 90@4 foundation stops at 93-95, so crossing it needs an informed multi-pair move
or an exact search over first layers, not random swaps; (2) at cap 4 a 300 s stall wastes the
restarts of BOTH arms (fresh roots reach only 92-96 in five minutes), so the cap-4 problem is the
restart policy more than the source; (3) the foundation idea does not transfer to InvMixColumns as
implemented, consistent with the why campaign's finding; (4) the mechanism stays available at zero
cost (default off) for an informed-move variant. Falsified under the tested conditions, not in
general: the pin length, the perturbation rule and the cap-4 restart timers were each tried at one
setting.

## 6. The single most justified next step

The proof side has earned it, the search side has not. All 280 open claims at G = 75 live in a
narrow region: hub cases A = 2..4 with no dark label (two to four missing labels meeting full
helpers), a configuration no real circuit exhibits. The sprint specified the finite enumerations
that decide such configurations with exact masks (joint hub systems over a fixed set of full
helpers; the Mt q = 1 and M q = 2 many-full-helper families) and never ran them; the October rows
add the class geometry (one output-bit class per repeated helper, 4 outputs per class) that makes
those enumerations small. Next step: one bounded proof agent (xhigh) to formulate and run the
exact realizability enumeration for the A >= 2 hub configurations behind the 229 load-bearing
keys, produce rows (or per-key exclusions) with written proofs, and certify; success criterion: 0
open claims at G = 75 together with regenerated certificates for the package's own 5,908 LP-only
exclusions at 75 (minutes of compute), which would establish L(M) >= 76; failure criterion: a
locally realizable A >= 2 configuration, which would locate exactly where new mathematics is
needed. On the search side, the only cheap follow-up with evidence is a cap-4 restart-policy
test (longer stalls or proposal-based stalls at cap 4), not a new campaign; no change tested in
this phase has earned a machine-scale 87 campaign.
