## 2. Strongest insights

**A. The 88 level and why the walk stalls (map/circuit property, measured).** Descent to 88 is cheap
(11 of 12 fresh chains by 25 min). At all 4,314 sampled 88 states the exhaustive one-value move has no
down-step (two independent runs); at every member of the 155 catalogued plateaus of <= 2e4 states too;
every RESOLVED +1 exit returns to 88 but 57-64 % of +1 descents were cut at a 400-state bound. The level
holds at least ~2,500-3,100 remove1 plateaus (independent-chain assumption), plausibly 3e4-5e5 (F12,
giants resolved); 97 % of visit mass is on plateaus never seen. The in-engine log (F30) shows a chain
entering 88 ~2,600 times per hour with 89 excursions of median 0.02 s; the 30 s sampler saw 3 % of
stays, so the hop and distinct-plateau rates quoted from samplers are instrument properties. Scope:
resistance to the tested moves under the tested conditions; not evidence that heuristic search cannot
find 87 (FINDINGS I.1). Prediction tested: hunt D's registered P1-P5/Q1-Q5 (F14): arrivals ratio not
established, distinct plateaus refuted, deeper landings confirmed, no 87, meeting count matches the
null model. Connectivity (F35, pre-registered, 12 workers x 1 h): trails are LOCAL: chains from different 88
seeds shared 0 plateaus against 24 expected under independent wandering, chains from the same seed
re-met all hour; the 20 ms 88 -> 89 -> 88 hops are mostly plateau-internal (39 of 40 catalogued).

**B. Every known bottom is a product of small factors; the 3-input-tree rule is MixColumns-specific.**
Plateaus are exact Cartesian products wherever closed (MC, InvMC, small analogues) and no bottom state
has a remove1 down-step on either map (F2, F28). On MC 88 each 3-way factor re-pairs an unshared
3-input tree (1,425 of 1,425); on InvMC the factors are "one consumer, choice of partner" including
4-input trees with a shared half. The vast level is MC-at-88-specific: InvMC's 118 is one lineage
found once, like 89@4 (F28) [measured; the depth-slack reading is a guess]. Coupled factors are
invisible to the remove1 fan (F31).

**C. 89@4 (checked twice).** 526 records are one cap-4 plateau of 81 circuits over a 53-value core; its
26-pair first layer is shared by none of 5,798 distinct 90@4 states, necessary but not sufficient (of
26 first-layer-equal 90@4 states, the 8 run-final traps do not connect, 11 transient ones do); the cap
halves the neutral moves, which is what "sealed" means; two entry events ever, never from NIST's 90@4;
uncapped the family is no trap. Exact: over the 89's pairs plus its family's level-2 vocabulary, G >= 89
is SAT-proved; over the first layer alone, open (one sub-scope closed: no 88 there without a
two-helper output recipe) (F15, F22).

**D. The proof side (proved, checked, certified unless marked).** The 75 proof's rows never mention 74:
the drift budget grows by 2 per gate and at 75 the rows leave 1,508 of 7,416 oriented claims open; the
October campaigns reached 509 with integral witnesses (F16). Real 88 circuits violate no row, leave
every capacity row slack >= 10, and sit 0-7 below the drift budget, which has no slack for any circuit
of any size (F17, corrected). New universal rows with proofs citing package lemmas, each independently
checked and certified with exact integer Farkas certificates and two checkers: N1 and the G* family
(1,508 -> 1,313; 1,772 certificates), the hub lemma H1 and the dark-label split (-> 996; 4,523), the
exact hub capacity 3 - r and packing rows (-> 789; 4,950) (F18, F21, F27). A joint-hub lemma, the
finite fact that two limit hubs never share a missing label (two independent implementations), and
label-class rows give 789 -> 686, proved and independently checked, certified at 04:30 UTC (F32, F36). What remains is
realizability of configurations with several hubs on few full helpers (locally realizable, so the
count relaxation cannot forbid them) and the small-q many-full-helper families: global content. Odd helpers: any circuit with <= 89
gates has >= 3 (h = 0, 1, 2 cases proved/replayed; premise x >= 24 read) (F20). The group-sharing
identity is a tautology and yields no bound (F26). No published lower bound above 32 exists for this
matrix, so 75 and 93 are new relative to the literature (F19).

**E. Exact scoped refutations and their weight.** The value-set SAT formulation is sound (equivalence
via fresh splits, sound symmetry breaks); ~4,300 kept sets over 37 circuits, the mobile parts of four
3-way plateaus, the archive's near-misses: all UNSAT, 0 SAT (F13, F23, F31, F33). Caveat (F29): the
archive's identical instrument returned UNSAT on 17 of 17 control windows around circuits that provably
had an improvement, because improvements sit at mask distance ~42; our own slack-1 power control (F34) confirms it on
independent data: 0 SAT windows on 28 real anchors, no generated window contains any known
improvement, k_min median 42, likelihood ratio 1.0. These are rigidity theorems about kept sets, not
evidence against 87; power needs large scopes or a different formulation.

**F. Cross-map behaviour.** Maps over 8x8 matrices gained at NIST's depth in 16 of 20 one-to-four
worker-hour trials (about 10 beyond the 1-3-gate run-to-run spread); 4x4 maps 2 of 7 by one gate. On
the existing run data (F25): "unsearched depth band" is excluded as a general mechanism, 4x4 maps sit
at the floor the search reaches (fresh root, NIST's and ours converge within 0-1 gate in minutes),
prior worker-hours were equal across classes, and the 8x8 gains are the 2nd-5th hour of a log-decaying
curve from ordinary one-hour endpoints (165 / 27 / 5 gates removed in the first half / second half /
final quarter) [measured; reading tagged guess]. The crossmap numeric rule scored 2 hits, 2 beaten, 6
misses over 10 maps.
